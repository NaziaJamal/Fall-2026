Expression Grab

A fast, standing vocabulary game that replaces worksheet activity 6B.

When0:31–0:34, after 6A
Time3 minutes
StageStudy (vocabulary)
AimRecall + use 10 expressions accurately

Preparation (5 minutes before class)

  1. Print the card sheet (page 3) and cut out the 10 cards. Card stock lasts longer; you'll reuse them in Weeks 5 and 10.
  2. Before class, spread the cards face up on different surfaces around the room: a table, a shelf, a windowsill, a chair. They should be 2–4 steps apart, so she has to move.
  3. Have a phone timer ready, and keep this page with you for the situations and model sentences.

How to play

Let's stand up for this one. Around the room there are ten cards with today's expressions. I'll describe a work situation. Find the right card, pick it up, and tell me a sentence about your job using it. If the sentence is correct, you keep the card. You have two minutes. Let's see how many you can collect.
  1. Round 1 (2 minutes). Read the situations in the table on page 2, in any order. Daniela walks to the card, picks it up, and says a full sentence connected to her own work.
  2. Accuracy rule. She keeps the card only if the pattern is correct (for example, come across as, build rapport with). If it's wrong, say only “Check the pattern” and let her try once more. The pattern is printed on each card to help her.
  3. Count (15 seconds). Count her cards. Write the number in the baseline record (Section 9) as an extra note. She'll try to beat it in Week 5.
  4. Quick feedback (45 seconds). Ask: “Which two expressions will you really use this week? In which email or meeting?”
Speed matters less than the sentence. If she is fast but her sentences are generic (“It is important to stand out”), push for her real context: “Stand out from whom? Which product?” That's where the vocabulary sticks.

Make it easier

  • Put all cards on one table.
  • Give the first word of the expression as a hint.
  • Accept a sentence about any business, not only hers.

Make it harder

  • Fold the pattern line under, so only the expression shows.
  • Swap roles: she picks a card and describes a situation; you find it.
  • Ask for a question instead of a sentence (“Could you…”).

Reuse later: as a 1-minute Week 2 warm-up (cards face down, she turns one over and uses it), and mixed with new cards for cumulative review in Weeks 5 and 10. Compare her count with today's.

Situations to read aloud (with answers)

Twelve situations for ten cards: situations 11 and 12 reuse a card, so she can “win” it again if she missed it the first time. Model sentences are examples only; hers should be about her own work.

1You want a new client to trust you and feel comfortable working with you.build rapport withI always try to build rapport with new distributors before I talk about prices.

7After your recommendation, you add sales data and two customer examples.supporting detailsI include supporting details, like last quarter's sales figures, after my recommendation.
8You start with “Great to hear from you” and finish with “Let me know if I can help.”signals of goodwillSmall signals of goodwill make my emails to clients feel more personal.
