Class 2 · Chapter 1 in four ideas
CLASS 2 · 33–65 MIN · JIGSAW

The four big ideas of Chapter 1

Students teach these to each other. You only need to know them well enough to check and correct.

A

Integrity — legal is not enough (1.1)

Integrity = what you say and what you do match. Following the law is only the minimum; ethics goes further.

Example: the Hippocratic Oath — doctors have a professional code. Business has no single shared code yet.

Ask: Can something be legal but unethical? Give an example from your field.

B

Think long term (1.2, first half)

Focusing only on this quarter's profit is short-sighted. Society lets a business exist, so the business owes something back — the social contract.

Example: Toyota sold cars in the US at a small loss for years to build trust. Buffett: never do anything you wouldn't want on a newspaper's front page.

Ask: Was Toyota being ethical — or just smart?

C

Stakeholders, not only stockholders (1.2, second half)

Stockholders own shares. Stakeholders are everyone affected: workers, customers, community, environment… Treating them well builds goodwill (reputation, trust), which supports profit. CSR = doing more than the law requires.

Example: Equifax — data of nearly half the US population was exposed; executives sold stock before the news was public.

Ask: If a company is ethical only because it pays, is that still ethics?

D

One standard, not many (1.3)

Ethical relativism = "right and wrong depend only on the situation." The book says this is a problem: use one consistent standard everywhere, and people will trust you.

Example: your cousin asks to use your 25% employee discount.

Ask: Is adapting to local customs (like gift-giving) always relativism? (Help them separate manners from principles.)

How to run it (32 min)
  1. Vote: "Being ethical makes a company more profitable in the long run." True or false? (2 min)
  2. Home groups of 4: each person takes A, B, C or D. (1 min)
  3. Expert groups read and fill the Expert Brief. (12 min) Then compare. (3 min)
  4. Back home: each expert teaches for 2–3 minutes without reading aloud. (12 min)
  5. Vote again. Who changed? Why? (2 min)
Key words in simple English

ethics — standards of right behavior we choose to follow

integrity — your words and actions match

stakeholder — anyone affected by a business decision

stockholder / shareholder — someone who owns shares

compliance — following the law (comply with)

utilitarianism — judge by results: most good for most people

deontology — judge by duties and rules

virtue ethics — judge by character

social contract — unwritten deal between business and society

goodwill — the trust and good feeling people have toward a company

CSR — corporate social responsibility: doing more than the law requires

ethical relativism — "right and wrong depend only on the situation"

Watch for: stakeholder vs stockholder — say both slowly. Stress: in-TEG-ri-ty · dee-on-TOL-o-jee · u-ti-li-TAR-i-an-ism.
