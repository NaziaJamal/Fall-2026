Part B · Short answer 15 points

Answer in 2–3 sentences. You may use the sentence starters. (3 points each)

  1. What is the difference between a short-term and a long-term view of profit? Give an example.

    Starters: A short-term view focuses on … A long-term view … For example, …

    Short-term: focuses on profit this quarter or this year (1). Long-term: thinks about results years from now; honesty and trust (goodwill) bring profit over time (1). Example (1): J&J's Tylenol recall cost a lot but customers came back; Samsung rushed the Note 7 and lost more; or any clear workplace example.
  2. A company must decide whether to close a factory. What would a utilitarian look at? What would Kant look at?

    Starters: A utilitarian would look at … Kant would look at …

    Utilitarian (1.5): the results, which choice brings the most good and the least harm for the most people (count workers, owners, the town, customers). Kant (1.5): duty and the motive, whether the company is honest and whether it treats workers merely as a means (a tool) for profit; results don't decide.
  3. What is the difference between the ethical minimum and the ethical maximum? Give an example.

    Starters: The ethical minimum is … The ethical maximum is … For example, …

    Minimum: the least a company can do and still obey the law (1). Maximum: the best, most ethical action possible (1). Example (1): J&J removed all Tylenol from U.S. stores (maximum); Samsung first only warned washer owners (minimum), then gave refunds and free pickup (maximum); or a clear example of their own.
  4. What is the difference between etiquette and ethics? Give one example of each.

    Starters: Etiquette is about … Ethics is about … For example, …

    Etiquette: manners and customs, what is polite, which can change from culture to culture (1). Ethics: right and wrong, especially when other people can be harmed (1). Examples (1, both needed): etiquette, e.g., arriving late, how to greet, a small gift; ethics, e.g., bribery, lying to customers, unsafe work.
  5. Name two things that employers owe their employees. Choose one and explain why it matters.

    Starters: Employers owe their employees … and … This matters because …

    Any two (1 each), e.g., a safe workplace, a fair wage, respect and no harassment, privacy, the right to join a union, good working conditions. Explanation (1): e.g., safety protects workers' lives; fair pay respects their work and builds loyalty and productivity.

Part C · Case analysis 23 points

Read the case, then answer C1–C5.

Brightline Outdoor (a fictional company) is an American company that sells jackets for $140. Its website says: "Every jacket is made ethically." The jackets are made by a factory called Sunfield in another country. Sunfield has 1,200 workers. A check of the factory finds three problems:
  • In the busy months, workers work 70 hours a week. This is legal in that country.
  • Workers earn more than the minimum wage, but not enough to live on.
  • The factory puts dirty water into a river. Farmers use this river for their crops.
The factory manager says: "Long hours are normal here. If you make us change, you will go to a cheaper factory, and our 1,200 workers will lose their jobs."
Fixing the three problems would cost Brightline $1.10 more per jacket. Another factory offers to make the jackets cheaper.
  1. 4 pointsC1 · Stakeholders. Name four stakeholders in this case. Write the type of each one.

    Types: enabling (owners, government) · functional (workers, suppliers, customers) · normative (competitors) · diffused (media, community groups, the environment)

    StakeholderType
    Any four, 1 point each (½ for the stakeholder, ½ for the type). Examples: Brightline's owners or shareholders, enabling; the government of Sunfield's country, enabling; factory workers, functional; Sunfield (the supplier), functional; customers, functional; the cheaper factory or other jacket companies, normative; the farmers, diffused (accept functional with a reason); the river/environment, diffused; the media, diffused.
  2. 4 pointsC2 · Minimum, maximum and the website.
    (a) What is the ethical minimum for Brightline? What is the ethical maximum? (2 points)
    (b) Is the website claim "Every jacket is made ethically" honest? Why or why not? (2 points)
    (a) Minimum (1): change nothing, because nothing is illegal there; or move to the cheaper factory. Maximum (1): stay, and help pay for the changes (fewer hours, fair pay, clean water); check the factory often; tell the public the truth.
    (b) Not honest (1), with a reason (1): workers work 70 hours, pay is too low to live on and the river is polluted, so the claim hides real problems; this is like greenwashing. Accept "partly honest" with a clear reason.
  3. 3 pointsC3 · Culture. The manager says long hours are "normal here." Is this just a local custom (etiquette), or is it a question of ethics? Circle one and give a reason.   Custom  /  Ethics  /  Both
    Choice (1) and reason (2). Accept any choice with a clear reason. Strong answers: working hours can be a local custom, and some workers want the extra money, but 70-hour weeks can hurt health and safety, so it is also ethics. Bonus idea, not required: Nike also faced this problem in the 1990s and improved its factories.
  4. 4 pointsC4 · Frameworks. Choose two frameworks from the list. Write what each one would tell Brightline to do, and why.

    Utilitarianism (most good for most people) · Kant (duty; don't use people only as tools) · Rawls (protect the weakest) · Aristotle (be a good, honest person)

    FrameworkWhat should Brightline do? Why?
    2 points each: what Brightline should do (1) and a reason connected to the case (1). Utilitarian: stay and fix the problems, because 1,200 jobs and the farmers' crops matter more than $1.10 per jacket. Kant: workers and farmers are being used only as tools for cheap jackets, and the website claim is not honest, so fix the problems and tell the truth. Rawls: protect the weakest, the low-paid workers and the farmers, so raise pay and clean the water. Aristotle: an honest, wise company would find a balanced solution and not hide problems.
  5. 8 pointsC5 · Paragraph. Write one paragraph (120–150 words): What should Brightline do? Stay with Sunfield and ask for changes, move to the cheaper factory, or another idea?
    Include all six parts: 1 my position · 2 a reason · 3 a fact from the case · 4 a framework, used for this case · 5 the other side, and my answer to it · 6 a conclusion
    Starters: I believe Brightline should … · because … · For example, the case says … · From a ___ perspective, … · Some people may say … However, … · Therefore, …
    Rubric (8): Position (1) · Reason (1) · A fact from the case, e.g., 70 hours, not enough to live on, the river, $1.10, 1,200 jobs, the website claim (1) · Framework used for the case, not only defined (2) · The other side stated and answered (2) · Conclusion (1). Don't deduct for length if all parts are there.
    Model (about 130 words): I believe Brightline should stay with Sunfield and ask for changes. This is the fairest choice for the workers. For example, the case says workers work 70 hours a week and do not earn enough to live on, but the website says every jacket is made ethically. From a Kantian perspective, Brightline is using the workers only as tools for cheap jackets, and its website is not honest. Some people may say that changes will cost money and that the workers could lose their jobs. However, the changes cost only $1.10 for a $140 jacket, and moving to another factory would hurt the same workers much more. Therefore, Brightline should stay, help pay for the changes and make its website honest.
