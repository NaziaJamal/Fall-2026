Language support

The language of the courtroom: asking, conceding, rebutting, presenting.

Asking leading questions

  • Isn't it true that…?
  • Wouldn't you agree that…?
  • So you're saying that…?
  • The apology came two days late, didn't it? ↘
  • Can you show us the evidence for that?
  • Where does that figure come from?

Conceding (without losing)

  • That's a fair point. However…
  • I accept that…, but that doesn't change our verdict because…
  • You're right about the timing. What matters more is…

Rebutting · refusing the premise

  • I'd put it differently.
  • That's not quite what the evidence shows.
  • That assumes…, and we don't think that's true.

Presenting a verdict

  • Good morning. We are here to present our verdict on…
  • The apology itself was… (quote)
  • Measured against 4A, it…
  • The evidence shows that…
  • On balance, our verdict is…, because…
  • I'll now hand over to…

In the courtroom

  • Opposing counsel: "Thank you. Our first question is…"
  • Opposing counsel, at the end: "No further questions."
  • Witness: "Could you repeat the question, please?"
  • Witness: "My colleague will answer that."

Reporting what people said

  • said · stated · announced (neutral)
  • admitted · acknowledged (agrees it's true)
  • claimed · insisted (doubt)
  • denied (says it's false)

Say it right

  • tribuNAL · VERdict · EVidence
  • cross-examinAtion · conCEDE · reBUT
  • Tag question, falling tone ↘ = "I expect you to agree."

Stage 1 · Speed Debate Lines

Engage · React · – · two lines facing each other
How it works
  1. The class stands in two lines, facing each other. Line A agrees with the statement; Line B disagrees (whatever you really think).
  2. Line A speaks for 45 seconds. Line B answers for 45 seconds, starting with a concession: "That's a fair point. However…"
  3. Line B moves one place to the left. New partner, new statement. After rounds 2 and 4, the lines swap sides.

Stage 3 · Fishbowl: Two Rounds of Cross-Examination

Activate · Practice · – · inner circle and outer circle
How it works
  1. Round 1: your teacher, as the witness, gives a 60-second verdict: "The Domino's apology was a success." Four volunteers in the inner circle cross-examine for 3 minutes. Use the question chain you wrote in Stage 2.
  2. Round 2: two volunteers take the witness chair and defend the opposite verdict: "The Domino's apology was a failure." Four new volunteers cross-examine them for 3 minutes.
  3. Anyone in the outer circle may tap a volunteer's shoulder to swap in, or sit in the empty fifth chair to ask one question.
  4. The outer circle observes and fills in the checklist for both rounds.

2 · The roles

Expert witnesses

The presenting team

  • Present your verdict in 5 minutes.
  • Everyone speaks in the presentation.
  • In cross-examination, each member answers at least one question.
  • Concede fair points; always return to your verdict.
Opposing counsel

The questioning team

  • Argue the opposite verdict, only through questions.
  • One question at a time. No speeches.
  • Use leading questions and question chains.
  • Finish with: "No further questions."
The jury

Everyone else

  • Listen in silence. No phones.
  • After each hearing, fill in your ballot (below) in 1 minute.
  • Judge the evidence, not the people.
  • Your votes decide "Most Persuasive Case".

4 · The rules of the court

  1. Only opposing counsel asks questions during the hearing. The jury stays silent.
  2. Questions only: if opposing counsel starts a speech, the judge will ask, "Your question, please?"
  3. Every presenter answers at least one question.
  4. Strict timing for every team. It's fair for everyone.
  5. Respect: attack the evidence, never the person.
