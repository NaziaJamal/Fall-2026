Stage 2 · Study Name · Say it right

Tag questions: the tone decides

The apology came late, didn't it? ↘

Falling tone: "I'm sure this is true. Agree with me." This is the cross-examiner's tool.

The apology came late, didn't it? ↗

Rising tone: a real question. "I don't know; please tell me."

Also leading: Isn't it true that…? · Wouldn't you agree that…? · So you're saying that…?

Practice the falling version with your partner
3 minutes. Say both versions dramatically. Choral drill of the falling version three times. Students write the meanings on the worksheet.

Stage 2 · Study Name

Answering: concede, rebut, redirect

Concede

"That's a fair point."

"I accept that the apology came late…"

Rebut

"However, the evidence shows…"

"…but that doesn't change our verdict, because…"

Refuse the premise

"I'd put it differently."

"That assumes…, and we don't think that's true."

Conceding a fair point makes you more credible, not less, as long as you return to your verdict.

Language help drawer · "Conceding" and "Rebutting"
2 minutes. Point out that a leading question contains a premise (an assumption). You can accept it, partly accept it, or refuse it.

Stage 5 · Engage 11:10–11:15

The court is in session

Expert witnesses

The presenting team. 5 minutes, then answer every question. Each member answers at least one.

Opposing counsel

3 minutes of questions only. Argue the opposite verdict through your questions. No speeches.

The jury

Everyone else. Fill in a ballot: your verdict, how persuaded you were, the strongest evidence, the best question.

The judge keeps time and grades. The highest average jury score wins Most Persuasive Case.
