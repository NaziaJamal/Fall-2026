Midterm Rubric · The Verdict

GAME analysis3 points Separates the stated and real goal and audience; explains how the message and expression (tone, channel, timing, messenger) served or failed the real audience, with specific examples. Covers all four elements, but mostly describes rather than analyzes, or one element is thin. Covers only some elements, or the analysis is general and could fit any case. No GAME analysis.
Language and 4A3 points Quotes the original words accurately; names the technique doing the work (euphemism, word temperature, passive or active responsibility, hedging, slogan, image) and explains its effect. Apology cases: a 4A audit with a reason for each A. Quotes the message and names a technique, but the effect is explained only briefly; or the 4A audit is incomplete. Comments on the language in general terms ("it was bad", "it was emotional") without precise analysis. No language analysis.
Verdict, evidence and sources3 points States a clear verdict early; supports it with two or more specific pieces of evidence (numbers, reactions, outcomes); names three or more credible sources aloud, including one found independently; answers the strongest counter-point. Clear verdict with evidence, but evidence is general, or only two sources are named, or the counter-point is not addressed. The verdict is unclear or mostly opinion; one source or none named. No verdict.
What I would have done2 points 2 · A concrete, realistic alternative (a sentence, channel, messenger, timing or action), clearly linked to the real audience. 1 · An alternative that is vague ("they should have been more honest") or not linked to the analysis. — None given.
Delivery and register2 points 2 · Speaks from the key-word card with eye contact; clear signposting; professional register; finishes within 4:00 (by 4:30 at most). 1 · Some reading, frequent filler words, register lapses, or stopped at 4:30 before finishing. — Reads a script or a screen.
Answering questions2 points 2 · Answers directly and calmly, supports answers with evidence, concedes and rebuts where useful, and applies GAME or 4A to a new situation. 1 · Partial or general answers, avoids part of a question, or struggles to apply the frameworks to a new situation. — Can't answer.
  • Time: a 1-minute and a 30-second card are shown. At 4:30 the talk is stopped; parts not yet delivered get no credit.
  • Notes: one key-word card, 25 words maximum. Reading a script, phone or laptop scores 0 for delivery.
  • Half points may be given between levels.
  • Accuracy: a fact that is clearly invented or misquoted lowers the score for the criterion where it appears.
  • Absence: the syllabus make-up policy applies (notice before class and documentation).
