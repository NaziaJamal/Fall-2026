Part 2 · The preparation week
3

Supporting students during the week

  1. Mid-week reminder (optional), two or three days before the midterm:
    Hi everyone, a quick midterm check-in. By now you should have read your case and its starter sources and found at least one more source yourself. This week: finish the planner in the Student Guide, write your key-word card (25 words maximum), and rehearse aloud with the 4-minute timer at least three times. Also practice answering hard questions about your case: what is the strongest argument against your verdict? See you next week!
  2. Collect slides in one folder and put them in case-number order the night before.
  3. Common questions:
    • "Can I change my case?" No: every case is different, and the running order depends on it.
    • "Can I say it was both a success and a failure?" They must choose "mostly" one or the other, but they can and should admit the strongest point on the other side.
    • "Can I use AI?" For checking English and finding sources, yes; the talk must be their own words. Your questions will show whether they know their case.
    • "My talk is 6 minutes." Cut Part 1 to three sentences and keep only the two best pieces of evidence.

5

The speaker procedure (7 minutes)

Repeat 26 times
4:00Talk
2:00Your questions
1:00Changeover
  1. Talk. Start the 4:00 timer at the first word. Show "1 MIN" at 3:00 and "30 SEC" at 3:30. At 4:30, say "Thank you, time."
  2. Questions. Ask two or three questions from section 6. Start with the one that tests their weakest part. Stop each answer at about 40 seconds: "Thank you. One more question."
  3. Changeover. Thank the speaker and lead the applause. Say "Jury, one minute for your notes." Finish the marking slip and tick the speaker as done.

Choosing your questions

Question 1, the evidence check: ask about something they said, or something important they left out.

Question 2, the other side: ask about the strongest argument for the opposite verdict.

Question 3 (if time), apply it: a follow-up from section 7 that asks them to use GAME or 4A in a new way.

If something goes wrong

Running late: shorten the changeover and use the 4-minute buffer before the break. Ask two questions instead of three. Don't shorten talks.

A student freezes: stop the clock and say: "Take a breath. Look at your card. What's your verdict?" Then restart.

A student reads a script: say once: "Please put the paper down and talk to us." If they keep reading, delivery scores 0.

A student is absent: move on to the next case. A make-up happens only if the syllabus conditions are met.

    8

    People's Choice and awards (9 minutes)

    1. Vote (3 minutes). Every student writes one name on a slip of paper: the most persuasive speaker of the day (not themselves). Collect the slips and count them while you give feedback.
    2. Highlights (3 minutes). Two things the class did well and one to work on.
    3. Awards (2 minutes). People's Choice, Best Evidence, Best Answer Under Pressure. Awards are for recognition only.
    4. Close (1 minute). Say when grades and feedback will be ready.
    5. Running late? Announce the People's Choice result by email instead.
