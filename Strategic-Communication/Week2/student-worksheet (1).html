Language support

The language of the strategist. Use it every time you speak today.

Analyzing

  • The stated goal is…, but the real goal seems to be…
  • On paper, this is addressed to…; in reality, it reached…
  • From an audience perspective, the word "…" signals…
  • The main strategic problem is…
  • What's missing from this message is…

Choosing and justifying

  • We chose X instead of Y because…
  • We decided against X, mainly because…
  • The main advantage of this approach is…
  • The trade-off is…, but we think it's worth it because…
  • On balance, we'd go with…

Recommending

  • I'd recommend… / We'd strongly advise…
  • A more effective approach would be to…
  • The organization should consider…
  • If I were advising them, I'd…

Challenging diplomatically

  • I'm not entirely convinced that…
  • What evidence supports that assumption?
  • But wouldn't that risk…?
  • How would you respond if…?
  • Can you walk us through how that would work in practice?

Presenting (signposting)

  • We've been asked to… Our recommendation in one sentence is…
  • Let me start with the goal. / Moving on to our audience…
  • I'll now hand over to [name], who'll explain…
  • The biggest risk we see is… and here's how we reduce it.
  • To sum up, …

Responding under pressure

  • That's a fair question. The short answer is…
  • I understand the concern. What we're doing about it is…
  • If I may clarify…
  • Let me address that directly.
  • I don't have that figure with me, but what I can tell you is…
  • What matters most here is… (bridge back to your key message)

Say it right

  • belLIGerent · emPHATically · re-aCCOMmodate
  • responsiBILity · mitiGAtion · EUphemism · STRATegy → straTEgic
  • a REcord (noun) → to reCORD (verb) · an OBject → to obJECT

Stage 4 · Strategic Choice Battles

Activate · Practice · – · teams of 3–4
How a battle works
  1. Your teacher gives your team a dilemma card. You have 3 minutes to choose A or B and plan your defense with GAME.
  2. 45 seconds: one speaker defends your choice: "We chose A instead of B because…" (Use at least two GAME letters.)
  3. Challenge: the opposing team asks one tough question. A different team member answers in 30 seconds.
  4. The class votes for the strongest defense, not the option they like. Speakers rotate every round.
  5. Round 2 twist: you receive another team's card and must defend the option they rejected.

Your team for the battles is your Assignment 1 team for the rest of today.

Stage 9 · Red Team Rehearsal

Activate · Practice · – · two teams together
What to do
  1. 2 minutes: Team 1 gives a fast version of its pitch: message, audience and your three choices.
  2. 2 minutes: Team 2 is the Red Team. Attack the plan with hard questions (use the question bank). Find the weakest point.
  3. Swap roles and repeat.
  4. 4 minutes: back in your own team, fix the weakest point.
Red Team question bank
  • Who exactly is your audience? That sounds like everyone.
  • How will you know it worked? Likes don't count.
  • What if this message reaches the wrong people?
  • Why this channel and not another one?
  • What's the biggest risk, and why didn't you mention it?
  • Your budget has just been cut in half. What do you drop?
  • A critic calls your campaign tone-deaf. What's your response?
  • Can you say your message in one sentence?
  • What would make this campaign fail?
