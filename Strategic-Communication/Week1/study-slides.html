Stage 5 · Name it

Euphemism /ˈjuːfəmɪzəm/

What it is

An indirect word or phrase for something embarrassing or unpleasant, often used to make it seem more acceptable.

Its cousin: connotation

The idea a word suggests in addition to its main meaning. "Re-accommodate" suggests a routine re-booking.

Soft wordWhat people saw or felt
re-accommodatedragged off the plane
let gofired
restructuringjob cuts

The risk

When the audience has already seen the reality, a soft word sounds like hiding it.

Definitions paraphrased from Oxford Learner's Dictionaries: euphemism · connotation

Ask: "Who was this word protecting — the passenger, the staff, or the company?" Euphemisms aren't always wrong (we say "passed away" kindly); the problem is a soft word for something everyone has watched on video.

Stage 5 · One word · model answers

What could he have said?

"I apologize for having to re-accommodate these customers."
→
"We are deeply sorry that a passenger was hurt on our flight. We are contacting him directly, and we are reviewing what happened."

Why didn't he say it? Possible reasons

Legal caution about admitting fault · wanting to protect his staff · writing for the wrong audience — the company, not the public who saw the video

Compare: his third message, a day later, called it a "truly horrific event" and apologized to the passenger.

Source: AP via Boston.com

The model sentence is a suggestion, not a quote. Accept any version that names the harm, addresses the person and states an action.

Stage 5 · Part 2 · discuss

Can communication be strategic and dishonest?

The profession's answer

The Public Relations Society of America's Code of Ethics lists six values: advocacy, honesty, expertise, independence, loyalty, fairness.

Honesty means…

Holding "the highest standards of accuracy and truth" when representing a client and talking to the public.

In this course, a strong analysis asks two questions: Did it work? and Was it honest?

Source: PRSA Code of Ethics

Quick whole-class question: "Is it acceptable?" Let two or three people disagree. Use the phrase bank: "It was effective, but was it honest?"

Stage 6 · compare

What researchers say

Strategic communication is "the purposeful use of communication by an organization to fulfill its mission."

— Hallahan, Holtzhausen, van Ruler, Verčič & Sriramesh (2007), International Journal of Strategic Communication, 1(1), 3–35
