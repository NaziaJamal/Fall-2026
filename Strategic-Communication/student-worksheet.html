Language support

The language of the courtroom: asking, conceding, rebutting.

Asking leading questions

  • Isn't it true that…?
  • Wouldn't you agree that…?
  • So you're saying that…?
  • The apology came two days late, didn't it? ↘
  • Can you show us the evidence for that?
  • How do you explain the fact that…?

Conceding (without losing)

  • That's a fair point. However…
  • I accept that…, but that doesn't change our verdict because…
  • You're right about the timing. What matters more is…
  • Partly, yes. The full picture is…

Rebutting

  • I'd put it differently.
  • That's not quite what the evidence shows.
  • With respect, the figures suggest the opposite.
  • Let me finish my point.
  • That assumes…, and we don't think that's true.

Presenting a verdict

  • Our case concerns…
  • The apology itself was… (quote)
  • Measured against 4A, it…
  • The evidence shows that…
  • On balance, our verdict is…, because…

Reporting what people said

  • said · stated · announced (neutral)
  • admitted · acknowledged (agrees it's true)
  • claimed · insisted (doubt)
  • denied (says it's false)

Say it right

  • tribuNAL · VERdict · EVidence
  • cross-examinAtion · conCEDE · reBUT
  • Tag question, falling tone ↘ = "I expect you to agree."
  • Tag question, rising tone ↗ = "I really don't know."

Stage 1 · Speed Debate Lines

Engage · React · – · two lines facing each other
How it works
  1. The class stands in two lines, facing each other. Line A agrees with the statement; Line B disagrees (whatever you really think).
  2. Line A speaks for 45 seconds. Line B answers for 45 seconds. Begin your answer with a concession: "That's a fair point. However…"
  3. Line B moves one place to the left. New partner, new statement. Every second round, the lines swap sides.

Stage 3 · Fishbowl: Cross-Examine the Teacher

Activate · Practice · – · inner circle and outer circle
How it works
  1. Your teacher gives a 60-second verdict: "The Domino's apology was a success."
  2. Four volunteers sit in the inner circle as opposing counsel. They have 3 minutes to cross-examine. Anyone in the outer circle can tap a volunteer's shoulder to swap in.
  3. The outer circle observes and fills in the checklist.
The best question asked (and why it worked)
A question that failed (too open? a speech, not a question?)
A moment the teacher conceded a point
How the teacher brought it back to the verdict

Stage 5 · The Court Is in Session

Engage · – · whole class
Expert witnesses

The presenting team. 5 minutes to present your verdict. Then answer every question; each member answers at least one.

Opposing counsel

Your assigned opponent. 3 minutes of questions only — no speeches. Argue the opposite verdict through your questions.

The jury

Everyone else. Listen, then fill in a ballot: your verdict, how persuaded you were, the strongest evidence, the best question.

Your teacher is the judge: keeps time, keeps order, and grades. The jury's votes don't change the grade, but the team that persuades the most jurors wins "Most Persuasive Case".
