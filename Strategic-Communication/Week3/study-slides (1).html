Part A · Study · The non-apology autopsy

It looks like an apology. What's the trick?

I'm sorry if anyone was offended.Click to reveal the trick"If" makes the harm hypothetical, and the problem becomes the listener's sensitivity.
I'm sorry you feel that way.Click to reveal the trickSorry for the listener's feelings, not for the speaker's action.
I apologize, but the media has exaggerated this.Click to reveal the trick"But" cancels everything before it, and shifts the blame.
We regret that the situation occurred.Click to reveal the trick"Regret" isn't sorry; "the situation" is vague; it just "occurred".
We apologize for any inconvenience this may have caused.Click to reveal the trick"Any" and "may have" shrink the harm; "inconvenience" is often too small a word.
Mistakes were made.Click to reveal the trickPassive voice: nobody made the mistakes.
Pairs: name the trick, then rewrite it as a real apology (worksheet, Part 2)
Pairs work on the worksheet first (about 4 minutes), then click each line here to check. Take one rewrite per line. Samples are in the teacher guide, Stage 3.

Part A · Study · Word watch

Words that shrink an apology

if→ (delete)

"I'm sorry if…" → "I'm sorry."

you feel→ I did

Apologize for the action, not the feeling.

but→ and / full stop

End the apology. Explain later, if at all.

regret→ I'm sorry

Regret is a feeling; sorry is an apology.

any→ the

"…for the missed flights," not "any problems".

may have→ did

The harm happened. Say so.

the situation→ name it

"…charging you twice," "…the data leak".

inconvenience→ the real harm

A canceled wedding flight is not an inconvenience.

Part A · Study · Grammar spotlight

The passive voice: who did it?

Mistakes were made.
→
I made a mistake.
Customers' data was exposed.
→
We exposed our customers' data.
A passenger was removed.
→
Our staff removed a passenger.

be + past participle hides the doer. I / we + active verb shows who is responsible. When is the passive fair, and when is it hiding?

Worksheet · Stage 3, Part 3
4 minutes. Show the left column first and ask "Who did it?" before you reveal the right column (or just talk through it). Fair passive: the person responsible really isn't known yet, or protecting a junior employee. Hiding: when the organization uses it to avoid saying "we".

Part B · Study · Name

The source ladder

Top

The original: the apology itself (company statement, video, letter). Best for what they said, not for whether it was true.

Middle

Reliable reporting: news agencies and major outlets (AP, Reuters, NPR), business school case studies, with dates and named sources. Wikipedia: a map to find sources, not a source to cite.

Bottom

Don't rely on it: reaction videos, anonymous blogs, "famous quotes" sites, anything with no date, no author and no link.

Rule for A2: one source from the top, two from the middle. Never quote a quote you can't trace.

Part B · Study · Name

Reporting verbs show your stance

Neutral

said · stated · announced

"The airline announced new rules on Tuesday."

Agrees it's true

admitted · acknowledged

"The CEO admitted the company had known for months."

Doubt

claimed · insisted

"The company claimed no data was lost."

Says it's false

denied

"The spokesperson denied any wrongdoing."

Your verb is a judgment. "Claimed" tells the audience you don't fully believe it — so be ready to say why.
