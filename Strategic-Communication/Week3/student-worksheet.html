Language support

The language of apologies, verdicts and evidence.

Reacting to an apology

  • It sounds sincere because…
  • It sounds hollow / defensive / scripted to me.
  • What's missing is a clear…
  • The word "…" makes it sound as if…
  • I'd accept it if they had also…

Disagreeing in the jury room

  • I take your point, but the evidence suggests…
  • I'm not sure that's fair. Consider…
  • Can we separate the apology from what happened afterwards?
  • Let's agree on the facts first.
  • I'm still not persuaded. Here's why…

Giving a verdict

  • On balance, we'd call it a success / a failure / a mixed result.
  • It worked in the short term, but…
  • It succeeded with one audience and failed with another.
  • The apology itself was weak; the actions that followed were strong.
  • Our verdict rests mainly on…

Delivering an apology (4A)

  • Acknowledge: What happened was… / Yesterday, we…
  • Accept: This was our failure. / I made the decision, and I was wrong.
  • Apologize: I'm sorry for… / We are deeply sorry to…
  • Act: Starting today, we will… / By [date], every customer will…

Reporting what people said

  • Neutral: said, stated, explained, announced
  • Agrees it's true: admitted, acknowledged, conceded
  • Doubt: claimed, insisted, alleged
  • Says it's false: denied, rejected

Say it right

  • acKNOWLedge · aPOLogize · aPOLogy
  • sinCERE → sinCERity · acCOUNTable
  • compenSAtion · liaBILity · CREDible
  • Say "I'm SORry" with a falling tone ↘ to sound sincere.

Stage 2 · Jury Room: The Domino's Video

Engage · React → Question · – · alone, then juries of 4
The case. April 2009. Two employees at a Domino's franchise in Conover, North Carolina, filmed themselves mishandling sandwiches as a "prank" and posted the videos on YouTube. Within days, the main video had close to one million views. Domino's says no contaminated food reached customers. Both employees were fired and charged. Domino's USA President Patrick Doyle then posted his own video on YouTube.

Study · Notice → Name → Practice · – · pairs, then whole class

Part 1 · Four piles (pairs, 5 min)

These twelve sentences all come from apologies. Put them into four piles: sentences that do the same job go together. You decide what the jobs are.

Reveal: the 4A framework (wait for your teacher)
A1 · Acknowledge

Name what happened, clearly and specifically. No vague "situation" or "incident".

A2 · Accept

Take responsibility without blaming others or making excuses.

A3 · Apologize

Say sorry directly to the people who were harmed, and mean it.

A4 · Act

Commit to specific actions that repair the harm and stop it happening again.

GAME tells you how any message works. 4A tells you whether an apology is complete. Use both.

Stage 4 · Apology Audit Stations

Activate · Practice · – · teams, moving around the room
How the stations work
  1. Four station cards are on the walls. Your team starts at one and has 7 minutes per station. When the timer ends, move clockwise.
  2. At each station: read the card, score each A from 0 to 2 (0 = missing, 1 = partly there, 2 = clear and complete), and write your evidence.
  3. Predict the outcome and write your verdict on a sticky note for the Verdict Wall.
  4. After all four, your teacher reveals what really happened.

Stage 5 · The Apology Auction

Engage · React · – · teams
The crisis (invented): On Friday evening, a software update at FreshCart, a grocery delivery app, charged 40,000 customers twice for their orders. Some customers' bank accounts went overdrawn. Customers discovered it first and are posting screenshots online. FreshCart's CEO must respond.
How the auction works
  1. Your team has $1,000. Your teacher auctions twelve apology "moves", one at a time. Bids go up in $50 steps.
  2. 2 minutes before the auction: as a team, plan the most you would pay for each move.
  3. Some moves help the apology. Some hurt it. Every move has a hidden score, revealed after the auction.
  4. The team whose moves add up to the highest score wins.
