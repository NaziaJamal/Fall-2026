Engage Stage 1 · The reveal

The insight behind the ad

The insight

The agency's research found that about 60% of body-wash purchases were made by women.

The media plan

Launched online on Super Bowl weekend (Feb. 2010), then on TV, placed where couples watch together.

The result

Goal: +15% sales. By May 2010: sales up 60% on the year before. By July: doubled.

Talk to the person who decides, not only the person who uses.
Source: Wieden+Kennedy case study; Muse by Clios

Study Stage 2 · Name it

The layers of an audience

1 · Addressed (stated) audienceWho it openly speaks to → women ("Hello, ladies.")
2 · Decision-makerWho says yes or pays → mostly women (about 60% of purchases)
3 · End userWho uses it → men
4 · InfluencerWho shapes the decision → the men themselves; people sharing it online
5 · Unintended audienceWho saw it but wasn't the plan → millions of online viewers worldwide
6 · Hostile audienceWho may reject it → open: men who feel compared? rival brands' fans?
7 · Target (real) audienceWho the campaign most wants to move → couples: women who buy and men who agree to switch
Match your answers to the term box on the worksheet first
7 minutes. Pairs match first. Then ask a pair for each layer before clicking. Model the stress: AL-ien-ate, de-mo-GRAPH-ic, STAKE-hold-er.

Study Stage 2 · Language

We can't read minds: make careful inferences

Inferring

  • The evidence suggests that…
  • It seems likely that the real target was…
  • On the surface, it speaks to…, but…

Limiting a claim

  • I'd argue that…, although we can't be certain.
  • That doesn't necessarily prove that…
  • X happened after Y, but not necessarily because of it.

Your turn: say three sentences about Old Spice to your partner. Use a different frame each time.

Pairs5:00
7 minutes in total. 5 in pairs, then two volunteers say one sentence each to the class. Correct form gently: "It seems likely that" + a full clause.

Study Stage 3 · Apple's audience map

What did the evidence show?

AddressedEveryone watching the launch: possible iPad Pro buyers. The message: the thinnest iPad ever, with everything inside.
TargetCreative people: musicians, artists, filmmakers. Apple says it wants to "empower creatives all over the world."
HostileThe same creatives. They saw the tools of their craft destroyed, at a time of worry about tech replacing human work.
UnintendedCompetitors: Samsung turned the anger into its own "UnCrush" ad. Also the wider public following celebrity posts.

Intended message vs. received message

Apple meant: all this creative power fits in one thin device. Many creatives heard: technology destroys what you love. The message is what the audience hears, not what you meant.

Sources: Ad Age via 9to5Mac and Macworld, May 2024; Laptop Mag; Creative Bloq
5 minutes. Ask a team for each box before you click. Key point: the target and the hostile audience were the same people. Then ask: "The ad passed a million views. Did it fail? With whom?" This sets up Week 8.

Activate Stage 3 · The Face-off

You are Apple's communications team. It's Thursday morning, May 9, 2024. The ad is being criticized everywhere.

Teams 1–4 · Stand by it

Explain the intended message. Keep the campaign running, including on TV.

Teams 5–8 · Apologize and pull it

Say sorry today. Cancel the TV plan. Win back the creative community.

Prepare: a claim that names the audience that matters most · two reasons with evidence · one risk you admit · one challenge question. Team 9 = judges.

Prep6:00
6 minutes. Every member prepares one part to say. Team 9 (the pair) prepares two challenge questions for each side and decides what makes an argument "strong." Check the claims: they should name an audience, e.g. "Creative professionals matter most because…"

1 vs. 5 · 2 vs. 6 · 3 vs. 7 · 4 vs. 8 · judges move between tables

Format

  1. "Stand by it" team: 60 seconds
  2. "Apologize" team: one challenge question

Language

Stage 3 · The verdict

Judges: what were the two strongest arguments, and why?

What Apple actually did

Apologized on May 9 ("We missed the mark"), cancelled the TV plan, but left the ad online. About three weeks later, a new iPad Pro ad, "Worlds Made on iPad," showed animators creating with the iPad.

The question is never "Was it good?"
It's "Good for whom, and at what cost?"
Old Spice read its buyer. Apple misread its core audience.
7 minutes. Judges report first. Then click to reveal what Apple did and ask: "Which team's plan was closest? What does the new ad tell us about who Apple decided mattered most?" End with the line on the slide.

Study Stage 4 · Changing register

Informal ↔ formal

Heads up!
↔
I am writing to inform you that…
We're cutting…
↔
We will discontinue… / We are adjusting…
We know it's a pain.
↔
We recognize this may cause inconvenience.
Grab me if you have questions.
↔
I would welcome the opportunity to discuss…
Nobody's losing hours.
↔
There will be no reduction in staff hours.

Rule of thumb: lead with what this audience cares about most. Keep the facts the same.

Activate Stage 6 · Feedback

When you are the audience

Checklist

  • Register: did the tone fit us?
  • Our concern: did they speak to it early?
  • One message: can we repeat it in one sentence?
  • The question: was it answered directly?

Round 3 · Under pressure

  • I understand why you're concerned.
  • That's a fair question. The short answer is…
  • Let me address that directly.
  • What we can promise is…

Feedback like a consultant: "One thing that worked was… One thing to change is…"
