The Layers of an Audience

Who is really listening, and how to tell the difference

1The big idea

When we say "audience," we often imagine one group of people. In strategic communication, an audience usually has layers: the people a message speaks to are not always the people who decide, the people who use the product, or the people the communicator most wants to change.

The stated audience is who a message speaks to. The real audience is who it is designed to move, plus everyone else who will hear it.

A strategist asks two questions before every message:

  1. Who am I talking to?
  2. Who else will hear this, and how will they react?

Remember Week 2: United's CEO wrote an email to employees. It was published by the media, and everyone read it. There is no such thing as an internal message.

2The seven layers

Each layer comes with a question that helps you find it. The examples come from cases we studied: the Old Spice ad (2010), the United Airlines email (2017), and Apple's "Crush!" iPad ad (2024).

1 · Addressed (stated) audience

Ask: Who does the message openly speak to? Look at greetings ("Hello, ladies"), the word "you," the channel, and the topic.

Old SpiceWomen: "Hello, ladies."
UnitedUnited employees
AppleEveryone watching the iPad Pro launch

2 · Decision-maker

Ask: Who says yes, pays, signs, or votes? This is the person whose decision creates the result.

Old SpiceMostly women: about 60% of body-wash purchases
Brightline (fictional)The city council approves the budget
Everyday exampleA parent who pays for a child's phone plan

3 · End user

Ask: Who actually uses the product or service? The end user and the decision-maker are often different people.

Old SpiceMen
Brightline (fictional)Late-night bus riders
Everyday exampleThe child who uses the phone

4 · Influencer

Ask: Whose opinion shapes the decision-maker? Influencers do not decide, but they push the decision. They can be family members, colleagues, experts, journalists, or people sharing posts online.

Old SpiceThe men themselves; people sharing the ad online
Brightline (fictional)Local media telling riders' stories
Everyday exampleA friend who recommends a phone brand

5 · Unintended audience

Ask: Who will receive this message even though it was not designed for them? Unintended audiences can be a disaster or a gift.

UnitedThe public, after the email was published (a disaster)
Old SpiceMillions of online viewers who never buy body wash (free publicity)
Everyday exampleA screenshot of a private message

6 · Hostile audience

Ask: Who is likely to reject or attack this message? A hostile audience can be inside your stated audience. Planning for them early is part of strategy.

AppleMusicians, artists and filmmakers who saw their tools crushed
UnitedCustomers who were already angry about the video
Everyday exampleEmployees reading an announcement about job cuts

7 · Target (real) audience

Ask: Whose belief or behavior does the communicator most want to change? This is the layer that decides whether the message succeeded. It is usually found by looking at the evidence, not at the greeting.

Old SpiceCouples: women who buy and men who agree to switch
AppleCreative people: Apple says it wants to "empower creatives all over the world"
Brightline (fictional)Late-night riders, who must still get home

3Telling the layers apart

Some layers are easy to mix up. These pairs cause the most confusion:

PairThe differenceQuick test
Addressed vs. targetThe addressed audience is who you speak to. The target is who you want to move. They can be the same group, but in clever campaigns they often are not."If only this group changed, would the campaign succeed?"
Decision-maker vs. end userOne decides or pays; the other uses."Who takes out the wallet? Who opens the box?"
Decision-maker vs. influencerThe decision-maker has the final say. The influencer only shapes it."Can this person say no and stop it?"
Unintended vs. hostileUnintended describes who receives it. Hostile describes how they react. An unintended audience can be friendly, and a hostile audience can be the people you addressed."Were they planned for? Separately: do they oppose us?"

Three things to remember

  • One person can be in several layers. A woman who sees the Old Spice ad and buys it for her partner is the addressed audience, the decision-maker, and part of the target.
  • Layers can move. Late-night riders have little power, until a TV report tells their story. Then they become very influential.
  • Not every message has every layer. Use the layers as questions, not as boxes you must fill.

4How to find the real audience

Companies rarely announce their real target. Look for evidence:

EvidenceWhat it tells youExample
Where and when it appearsMedia placement shows who the company is paying to reach.Old Spice placed its ads where couples would watch together.
What is measuredThe success metric reveals the real goal.Old Spice measured body-wash sales, not only views.
What the company says laterInterviews, earnings calls, and case studies often reveal the strategy.Apple's apology: it wants to "empower creatives"
How the company reacts to criticismA fast apology shows which group the company cannot afford to lose.Apple apologized to creatives within two days.
The words and registerFormality, pronouns, and vocabulary show who the writer imagines.The four Brightline messages (Stage 4)

5Language for talking about audiences

Useful verbs and collocations

ExpressionExample
target (a group)The campaign targeted younger buyers.
address (a group)The ad addresses women directly.
speak to / appeal toThe humor appeals to both partners.
reachThe email reached a much wider audience than planned.
resonate withThe message resonated with younger viewers.
alienateThe film alienated some loyal customers.
core customersA rebrand must be careful not to lose its core customers.

Making careful claims about audiences

  • On the surface, the message speaks to…, but the real target seems to be…
  • The evidence suggests that the company was trying to reach…
  • It's likely that the decision-maker here is…, although the end user is…
  • The message may have been designed for…, but it also reached…
  • That doesn't necessarily prove that… (correlation isn't causation)

Common mistakes

AvoidSay insteadWhy
The ad addresses to women.The ad addresses women."Address" (meaning "speak to") takes no preposition.
The campaign targets to young people.The campaign targets young people. / is aimed at young people."Target" takes no preposition; "aim" takes "at."
The message resonated young people.The message resonated with young people."Resonate" needs "with."
They lost the audience's trusts.They lost the audience's trust."Trust" is uncountable.
The audiences was angry.The audience was angry.One audience = singular. Use "audiences" for different groups.
