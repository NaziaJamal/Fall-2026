Brightline Transit and Easton are fictional. They were created for practice.

Message W

FOR IMMEDIATE RELEASE · Brightline Transit to replace late-night Route 9 service with on-demand shuttle

EASTON, Feb. 3 — Beginning March 1, Brightline Transit will replace late-night bus service on Route 9 with Night Hop, an on-demand shuttle available after 11 p.m. The change follows a year-long ridership review. "Our goal is simple: to put our drivers where riders need them most," said General Manager Marcus Lee. No drivers will lose hours. Media contact: press office, Brightline Transit.

Message X

Heads up, night riders! From March 1, Route 9 buses will end at 11 p.m. Need a ride later? Book Night Hop in the Brightline app: door to door, seven days a week, just $3 a trip. We know change is a pain. Tell us how it goes. We're listening. #BrightlineEaston

Message Y

Team, a quick heads-up before you hear it anywhere else. From March 1, Route 9 will stop running after 11 p.m. Nobody on the late shift is losing hours: we're moving those shifts to Routes 4 and 12, where we've been short for months. Your supervisor will go through the new schedules with you this week. Questions? Grab me at the depot or reply to this email. Thanks for everything you do. — Dana, Operations

Message Z

Dear Members of the Transportation Committee: I am writing to inform you of a service adjustment on Route 9, effective March 1. Following a twelve-month review, ridership after 11 p.m. averaged fewer than four passengers per trip. Reallocating these hours to Routes 4 and 12 will allow us to address persistent staffing shortages at no additional cost to the city. A replacement on-demand service will be available to affected riders. I would welcome the opportunity to present the full review at your next meeting. Respectfully, Marcus Lee, General Manager

Change the Audience

Activate · Perform → Feedback → Perform again · 12:05–12:35 · two teams together
How a round works (8 min)
  1. The other team draws an audience card for you and becomes that audience.
  2. Your spokesperson has 1 minute to prepare, then delivers your message in 30–45 seconds.
  3. The audience asks one tough question. The spokesperson answers.
  4. The audience gives feedback with the checklist. Then switch roles.
A different person is spokesperson each round. Round 3 is a hostile audience.

Feedback checklist (when you are the audience)

  • Register: Did the tone and formality fit us?
  • Our concern: Did they speak to what we really care about, early?
  • One message: Could we repeat the main point in one sentence?
  • The question: Did they answer it directly, or avoid it?

Give feedback like a consultant: "One thing that worked was… One thing to change is…"

Language help

Identifying the audience

  • On the surface, the message speaks to…
  • The people who actually decide are…
  • The real target seems to be…
  • This message was never meant for…, but they saw it anyway.

Making a careful inference

  • The evidence suggests that…
  • It seems likely that the real goal was…
  • I'd argue that… although we can't be certain.
  • That doesn't necessarily prove that…
  • Correlation isn't causation: … happened after…, but not necessarily because of it.

Defending a choice

  • We chose this approach because…
  • The main advantage is…
  • We accept the risk that…, because…
  • The evidence on our side is…

Challenging diplomatically

  • I'm not entirely convinced that…
  • What evidence supports that assumption?
  • But wouldn't that risk alienating…?
  • How would you respond to customers who…?

Changing register

  • Heads-up → I am writing to inform you…
  • We're cutting… → We will discontinue… / We are adjusting…
  • We know it's a pain → We recognize this may cause inconvenience.
  • Grab me / Ask me → Please contact… / I would welcome the opportunity to…

Giving feedback

  • One thing that really worked was…
  • As your audience, I felt…
  • Next time, you might want to start with…
  • I wasn't sure what you wanted us to do.

Key words

  • alienate (/ˈeɪliəneɪt/) · to make people feel you are not on their side
  • resonate with · to connect emotionally with a group
  • backlash · a strong negative public reaction
  • core customers · the loyal people who buy most of your product
  • demographic (/ˌdeməˈɡræfɪk/) · a group defined by age, gender, income, etc.
  • stakeholder · anyone affected by a decision or able to affect it
  • register · the level of formality you choose for an audience
