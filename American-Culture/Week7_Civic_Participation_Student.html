Welcome to Week 7: Civic Participation

This week we learn how people take part in their community in the U.S. — by voting, speaking up, volunteering, and joining in. We focus on Boston and Massachusetts. You don't need to be a citizen to participate: there is a place for everyone!

Pair Activity

Can They Vote? (Massachusetts)

Read about each person with a partner. Can they vote in the next Massachusetts election? Choose Yes, No, or Not yet (they can, after they do something first). Write why.

Remember the rules: U.S. citizen · 18 or older on Election Day · Massachusetts resident · registered at least 10 days before the election.
1. Ana, 19

U.S. citizen, born in Puerto Rico. Lives in Dorchester. Registered online last year.

2. Kenji, 25

International student from Japan with a student visa. Lives in Allston and loves volunteering.

3. Fatima, 17

U.S. citizen in East Boston. She turns 18 two weeks before Election Day. She pre-registered at 16.

4. Carlos, 40

Green card holder (permanent resident) for 10 years. Pays taxes and lives in Jamaica Plain.

5. Linh, 30

Became a U.S. citizen at a ceremony after the registration deadline. The election is in 3 days. She is not registered.

6. David, 45

U.S. citizen. Just moved to Boston from New Hampshire. Registered to vote in New Hampshire only. The deadline has not passed.

  1. Ana — Yes. She is a citizen, over 18, a Massachusetts resident, and registered. (People born in Puerto Rico are U.S. citizens.)
  2. Kenji — No. He is not a U.S. citizen. He can still volunteer, attend meetings, and speak at public comment!
  3. Fatima — Yes. She will be 18 on Election Day, and she pre-registered (Massachusetts allows pre-registration at 16).
  4. Carlos — No. Green card holders are not citizens yet. He could apply for citizenship (naturalization).
  5. Linh — Not yet. New citizens can register in person at the local election office until 4 p.m. the day before the election, with proof of the ceremony date.
  6. David — Not yet. He must register in Massachusetts (his new home) at least 10 days before the election.

Exercise 2

Reading — Getting Involved, One Step at a Time

Maria moved to Boston two years ago. At first, she thought "civic participation" only meant voting — and since she isn't a citizen yet, she believed there was nothing she could do. Then her son's school invited families to a meeting about the school lunch program.

Maria went, listened, and even asked a question about the lunch menu. A few months later, she went to a City Council hearing about a new park near her house. During the public comment time, she shared her opinion, and the councilors thanked her for coming.

Maria still isn't eligible to vote, but she has learned that civic participation has many doors — and she is walking through several of them.

  1. She thought civic participation only meant voting, and she isn't a citizen yet.
  2. She went to a school meeting and asked about the lunch menu. She also spoke during public comment at a City Council hearing.
  3. She shared her opinion about a new park during public comment, and the councilors thanked her.
  4. Civic participation has many forms, and everyone — even people who can't vote — can take part.

Study Notes: Civic Participation in Boston

Read these notes after class to review. Click a question at the bottom to test yourself.

1. What is civic participation?

Civic participation means all the ways people take part in their community and government. There are many "doors":

Votingchoosing leaders and deciding questions
Speaking upsharing your opinion at meetings or by email
Volunteeringhelping your community without pay
Joining ingoing to meetings, events, and groups

2. Who can vote in Massachusetts?

  • You must be a U.S. citizen.
  • You must be 18 or older on Election Day. (You can pre-register at 16.)
  • You must live in Massachusetts.
  • You must register at least 10 days before the election. You cannot register on Election Day.
Important: Only U.S. citizens can register and vote. Green card holders and international students cannot vote. Never register to vote if you are not a citizen — it can cause serious immigration problems. If you are not sure, ask an immigration lawyer or legal aid office.
New citizens: If you became a citizen after the deadline, you can still register in person at your local election office until 4 p.m. the day before the election. Bring proof of your ceremony date.

3. How to register (if you are eligible)

  1. Check that you are eligible.
  2. Fill out the form: online (you need a Massachusetts driver's license or state ID), by mail, or in person (at the Boston Election Department in City Hall, or at the RMV).
  3. Submit it at least 10 days before the election.
  4. Wait for your confirmation notice in the mail.

Check your registration status: sec.state.ma.us

4. Three levels of government

LevelLeaderExamples
National (U.S.)President, Congresspassports, green cards, Social Security, the military
State (Massachusetts)Governor, the State Housedriver's licenses (RMV), state taxes, state laws
Local (City of Boston)Mayor, City Councilstreets, trash, parks, police, fire, public schools

Local problems like potholes, broken streetlights, or missed trash: call or use the Boston 311 app.

5. Boston City Council

  • 13 councilors: 9 represent districts (parts of the city) and 4 represent the whole city ("at-large").
  • They are elected every two years. They make local laws and approve the city budget.
  • Anyone can testify at a hearing: in person, on Zoom, by email, or with a video — in any language. Keep it to about 2 minutes.

6. Boston School Committee

  • Makes decisions about Boston Public Schools.
  • In Boston, the Mayor appoints the 7 voting members (plus 1 student member). In most other Massachusetts towns, people elect their school committee.
  • Parents and community members can speak at meetings during public comment.

7. Useful language

Public comment"Good afternoon. My name is ___, and I live in ___. I am here to support ___ because ___."
Agreeing"I agree with ___ because…" · "That's a good point."
Disagreeing politely"I see your point, but…" · "I understand, however…"
Comparing"In my home country, ___, but here ___." · "Both countries…"

8. Test yourself

1. What are four ways to take part in civic life?

Voting, speaking up, volunteering, and joining in (going to meetings and events).

2. What is the voter registration deadline in Massachusetts?

10 days before the election. You cannot register on Election Day.

3. Can a green card holder vote?

No. Only U.S. citizens can vote. But green card holders can volunteer, attend meetings, and give public comment.

4. How many members does the Boston City Council have?

13 — 9 district councilors and 4 at-large councilors.

5. Who chooses the Boston School Committee?

The Mayor appoints the 7 voting members.

6. Your street has a big pothole. Who do you contact?

Local government — the City of Boston (Boston 311).
