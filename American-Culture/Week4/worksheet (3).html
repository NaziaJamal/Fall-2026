Exercise 4Reading Comprehension

Read the passage, then answer the questions in complete sentences.

American schools have changed enormously over time. In the 1700s and 1800s, many children studied together in a single "one-room schoolhouse," where one teacher taught students of all ages — sometimes from six years old to sixteen. In 1837, an educator named Horace Mann began the "Common School Movement," arguing that every child, rich or poor, deserved a free public education. His ideas helped create the public school system Americans use today.

But for many years, equal education was not available to everyone. In many states, Black and white students were forced to attend separate schools, a practice called segregation. This changed in 1954, when the Supreme Court decided the case Brown v. Board of Education, ruling that segregated public schools were unconstitutional. This decision was a major victory for the Civil Rights Movement and still influences conversations about equality in schools today.

Today, American students move through several stages: Elementary School (Kindergarten–Grade 5), Middle School (Grades 6–8), and High School (Grades 9–12). After graduating high school, many students choose to continue their education at a community college, a four-year college or university, or move directly into the workforce.
