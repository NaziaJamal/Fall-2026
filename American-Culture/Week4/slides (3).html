A Look Back

The One-Room Schoolhouse

1600s – early 1900s

In early America, one teacher taught students of all ages, from 6 to 16, in a single room — often heated by a wood stove.

In 1837, Horace Mann led the "Common School Movement," arguing every child — rich or poor — deserved a free public education.

Talk about it · PairsDid your country ever have small, one-teacher schools? How has education changed there?
A Look Back

The Fight for Equal Schools

Until the 1950s, many states forced Black and white students to attend separate schools — a system called segregation.
In 1954, the Supreme Court case Brown v. Board of Education ruled that segregated public schools were unconstitutional.
The ruling was a landmark step in the Civil Rights Movement and still shapes conversations about equality in schools today.

Types of Schools

Public, Private & Homeschool

Public School

Funded by the government — free for all students. Most American children (more than 8 in 10) attend public school.

Private School

Paid for with tuition; often religious or independent. Fewer than 1 in 10 students attend, and many wear uniforms.

Homeschool

Children are taught at home by parents or tutors. A growing option for about 3 million U.S. students.

Did You Know?

Why Are School Buses Yellow?

In 1939, a national conference chose "National School Bus Glossy Yellow" as the standard color for school buses.

Yellow is easy to see in early morning light and fog, and black letters show up well on it — keeping children safer.

School Traditions

Prom, Homecoming & Yearbooks

Homecoming

A fall tradition welcoming back former students (alumni), with a football game, a parade, and a dance.

Prom

A formal dance for older students (usually juniors & seniors), famous for fancy dresses, tuxedos, and a creative invitation called a "promposal."

Yearbook

A printed book of photos and memories from the school year, signed by classmates.

Did You Know?

School Can Close for Snow

In snowy parts of the U.S., schools sometimes cancel classes for a "snow day" when roads are too dangerous.

Many students hope for one by wearing pajamas inside-out or putting a spoon under their pillow — a popular superstition!

Talk about it · PairsDo students in your country have any school superstitions?
School Life

Sports Are a Very Big Deal

Unlike in many countries, American schools have their own sports teams — football, basketball, baseball, track.
Big high school football games can draw thousands of fans, with marching bands, cheerleaders, and a school mascot.
Students also join clubs like band, debate, robotics, or student government — these matter a lot for college applications.
School Life

Every School Has a Mascot

Team Spirit

Schools choose an animal or character (Eagles, Tigers, Bulldogs) to represent their teams.

School Colors

Every school has official colors, worn proudly on T-shirts, banners, and face paint at games.

Spirit Days

Students dress up in themed outfits (Pajama Day, Twin Day) to show school pride together.

How Students Are Graded

Letter Grades & the GPA

A90–100%GPA 4.0
B80–89%GPA 3.0
C70–79%GPA 2.0
D60–69%GPA 1.0
FBelow 60%GPA 0.0
GPA (Grade Point Average) is one number, usually 0.0–4.0, that summarizes a student's grades. Colleges use it to compare applicants.

Grading scales can vary slightly from school to school.

After High School

Higher Education Choices

Community College
2-year programs (Associate's Degree). Affordable — many students transfer to a 4-year university later.
College / University
4-year programs leading to a Bachelor's Degree — the most common path after high school.
Graduate School
Optional further study for a Master's or Doctoral (PhD) degree, usually 2+ more years.

Did You Know?

Lunch Trays & the Cafeteria

Most students eat lunch at school in a large room called the cafeteria, often carrying a tray with sections.
Students buy a hot lunch or bring one from home in a "brown bag" or lunchbox — some get free or reduced-price meals.
Where you sit matters! Cafeteria tables and friend groups are famous in American school movies and TV shows.
Did You Know?

Mornings Start With a Pledge

Many U.S. classrooms begin the day by standing, facing the flag, and saying the "Pledge of Allegiance," a short promise of loyalty to the country.

Other traditions include morning announcements, spirit days, and the national anthem at sports events.
