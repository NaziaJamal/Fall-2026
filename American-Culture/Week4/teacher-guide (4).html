Learning Objectives

By the end of this lesson, students will be able to:

  • Describe the stages of American education from kindergarten to graduate school
  • Explain two key moments in U.S. school history (Horace Mann and Brown v. Board of Education)
  • Talk about American school traditions such as prom, homecoming, mascots, and the Pledge
  • Understand letter grades and calculate a simple GPA
  • Use school vocabulary (freshman, senior, recess, locker, major…) accurately
  • Compare American schools with schools in their home country in speaking and writing

Step-by-Step Procedure

1. Welcome & Goals 2:00–2:05 Slides 1–2

  • Introduce the topic. Ask: "What do you already know about American schools — maybe from movies or TV?" Take 2–3 quick answers.

2. Find Someone Who NEW 2:05–2:20 Slide 3 · Worksheet Warm-Up

Purpose: Get everyone moving, speaking, and remembering their own school days.

  • Model the question form on the board: "Did you ever wear a uniform to school?" → "Yes, I did!" / "No, I didn't."
  • Students stand up, mingle, and write one name per row on the worksheet, plus one detail from a follow-up question. Start the 10:00 timer.
  • Debrief (3 min): "Who found someone who had classes on Saturday? Where was that?"

3. School Structure + Vocabulary Matching 2:20–2:35 Slide 4 · Worksheet Ex. 1

  • Walk through the four stages. Concept check: "How old is a typical student in Grade 7?" (About 12–13, in middle school.)
  • Students complete Exercise 1 alone (5 min), then click Check. They compare tricky items with a partner.
  • Pronunciation: FRESH-man, SOPH-o-more, ca-fe-TE-ri-a, MAS-cot.

4. History 2:35–2:55 Slides 5–6

  • Slide 5: Explain the one-room schoolhouse and Horace Mann. Pairs then discuss the "Talk about it" question (3:00 timer). Take 2–3 shares.
  • Slide 6: Present Brown v. Board of Education. Teach segregation, unconstitutional, and Supreme Court.
Sensitivity note: Present segregation factually and respectfully. Some students may come from places with their own history of separation by race, religion, caste, or language. Let students choose whether to make connections. Don't call on anyone to speak for their group.

5. By the Numbers: Guess First NEW 2:55–3:05 Slide 7

  • Pairs write down a guess for each of the four questions (2 min).
  • Reveal one card at a time. The pair with the closest guess for each card wins a point.
  • Language focus: "We guessed about… / We were close! / We were way off!"

6. Types of Schools 3:05–3:15 Slide 8

  • Explain the three types, then run the class vote. Ask 2–3 students from different choices to explain why.

7. Reading Comprehension 3:15–3:40 Slide 9 · Worksheet Ex. 4

  • Students read silently, then answer questions 1–5. Start the 20:00 timer.
  • When they click Check, each answer shows the key points they included (✓) or may be missing (○), plus sample answers.
  • Use the last 5 minutes for pairs to compare answers. Go over question 5 (the opinion question) as a class if there's time.

Break 3:40–4:10 Slide 10

8. Would You Rather: School Edition NEW 4:10–4:25 Slides 11–15

Purpose: Re-energize the class after the break with movement and quick opinion language.

  • Slide 11 explains the rules. Board the frames: "I'd rather … because …" / "I prefer … to …"
  • For each prompt (Slides 12–15), students move LEFT or RIGHT, then talk with someone on their side (1:00 timer). Each side shares one reason with the class.
  • About 3 minutes per prompt. If one side is empty, ask a volunteer to play "devil's advocate" for that side.

9. Buses, Traditions & Snow Days 4:25–4:40 Slides 16–18

  • Slide 16: Pairs answer the transportation question (2 min).
  • Slide 17: Ask: "Which tradition sounds the most fun? Do you have something similar?"
  • Slide 18: Pairs share school superstitions from their countries. Take 2–3 fun examples.

10. Sports, Mascots → Design Your Dream School NEW 4:40–5:05 Slides 19–21 · Worksheet Group Activity

  • Slides 19–20 (5 min): Ask: "Did your school have a mascot or team colors?"
  • Slide 21: Put students in groups of 3–4. They fill in the worksheet planner (school name, mascot, colors, Spirit Day, a new rule). Start the 10:00 timer.
  • Pitches (10 min): Each group has 1 minute. The class votes by show of hands, and groups can't vote for themselves.

11. Grades & GPA Challenge NEW 5:05–5:15 Slides 22–23

  • Explain the A–F scale and the GPA. Mention that the exact scale can vary from school to school.
  • On Slide 23, pairs calculate Alex's and Maria's GPAs (3 min), then click to reveal. Answers: Alex = 3.2, Maria = 3.5.
  • Language: "Maria has a higher GPA than Alex." This is good comparative practice.

12. College, Costs, Cafeteria & the Pledge 5:15–5:27 Slides 24–27

  • Move briskly, about 3 minutes per slide. On Slide 25, pairs answer: "Is university free or expensive in your country? Who pays?"
  • Teach tuition, scholarship, student loan, and in-state.

13. Vocabulary Review 5:27–5:37 Slide 28 · Worksheet Ex. 2 & 3

  • Review the eight words on Slide 28. Students complete Exercises 2 and 3 and click Check after each one.

14. Discussion Roulette & Close NEW 5:37–5:50 Slides 29–30

  • Put students in groups of 4 and give each person a role: Leader, Timekeeper, Reporter, Encourager.
  • Click Spin. Every group discusses the same question (3:00 timer), and Reporters share one answer. Repeat 2–3 times.
  • Slide 30: Assign Exercise 5 as homework. Students choose one prompt and write 4–5 sentences.

Timing Tips

  • Running behind? Use only 2 Would You Rather prompts, and shorten the Design Your Dream School pitches to 30 seconds per group.
  • Ahead of schedule? Add more Discussion Roulette spins, or have each student invent a GPA "report card" for a partner to calculate.
  • Large class? For Find Someone Who, ask students to fill in at least 5 rows instead of all 8.

Common Student Mistakes

  • "I'm a senior" (age): in school, senior means a final-year student. It's also a polite word for an older person, so check the context.
  • Grade vs. class: "I'm in seventh grade" (level), not "I'm in seventh class."
  • College vs. university: Americans often say "college" for any university: "She's in college."
  • "Would rather" + base verb: "I'd rather wear a uniform", not "I'd rather to wear" or "I'd rather wearing."
  • Did you ever + base verb: "Did you ever ride a bus?", not "Did you ever rode."

Exercise 4 — Reading Comprehension (sample answers)

  1. A one-room schoolhouse was a small school with one room and one teacher. Students of all ages, from about six to sixteen, studied there together.
  2. Horace Mann was an educator who started the Common School Movement in 1837. He argued that every child, rich or poor, deserved a free public education.
  3. The Supreme Court decided that segregated public schools were unconstitutional.
  4. Elementary School (Kindergarten–Grade 5), Middle School (Grades 6–8), and High School (Grades 9–12).
  5. Answers will vary. Look for a clear reason, for example: it gave all students the right to study together and was a big step toward equality.

Exercise 5 — Writing (what to look for)

  • Prompt 1: at least 4 sentences, two similarities and two differences, and comparison words (both, but, however, while, unlike).
  • Prompt 2: a named tradition, at least two reasons, "I would…" or "If I were…", and vocabulary from the lesson.
