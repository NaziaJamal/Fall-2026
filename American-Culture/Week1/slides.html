Short Reading

Worksheet 1B
Read the text. Then answer the questions with your partner · 8 minutes
What Do Americans Believe In?

The United States is a country built on big ideas. From its earliest days, Americans have believed in freedom — the right to speak, to choose, and to live as you wish. Alongside freedom, Americans deeply value hard work. There is a popular belief called the “American Dream” — the idea that any person, no matter where they come from, can succeed if they work hard enough.

Americans also believe strongly in equality. The Declaration of Independence says that “all men are created equal.” Religion is also important to many Americans — the U.S. has people of many faiths. Finally, independence is a core value: many Americans are raised to take care of themselves, make their own decisions, and be self-reliant from a young age.

Of course, not every American thinks the same way. The U.S. is a very large and diverse country.

1. In your own words, what is the “American Dream”?
2. Which value is NOT in the reading?
3. Which value is most similar / different in your country?
4. Challenge: Is the American Dream possible for everyone?

True or False?

Worksheet 2B
Stand up for TRUE · Stay seated for FALSE · Then discuss WHY!
1
When Americans say “How are you?”, they want to hear about your health in detail.
→ FALSE — It's a greeting. Just say “Good, thanks! And you?”
2
Arriving 10 minutes late to a business meeting is totally fine in the U.S.
→ FALSE — Be on time. “On time” often means 5 minutes early.
3
It is normal to smile and make brief eye contact with strangers on the street.
→ IT DEPENDS — Common in small towns and suburbs, less common in big cities.
4
At a sit-down restaurant, leaving a tip is optional — only if the food was good.
→ FALSE — A 15–20% tip is expected. It pays for the service, not the food.
5
Asking a new colleague “How much do you make?” is a great conversation starter.
→ FALSE — Salary is taboo. Try work, weekend plans, or the weather instead.

Dialogue — First Day at the Office

Worksheet 2C
Read with a partner. Switch roles. Then answer the questions on your worksheet.
Jake (colleague):Hey! You must be the new hire. I'm Jake. Welcome aboard! Mia (new employee):Hi Jake, I'm Mia. Nice to meet you. Jake:So, how are you settling in? Is this your first time in the U.S.? Mia:Yes, it is! Everything is a little different, but I'm excited. Jake:That's awesome. Hey, we're all going out for lunch — it's a company tradition on someone's first day. You in? Mia:Of course! Should I bring anything? Jake:Nope, just yourself! Oh, heads up — when we eat out, we usually split the bill. And don't forget the tip! Mia:Of course. How much should I leave? Jake:About 20% is standard. Oh, and one more thing — our morning meetings start at 9 sharp. Being on time is a big deal around here!
