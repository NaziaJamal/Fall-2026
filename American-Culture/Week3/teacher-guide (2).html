Learning Objectives

By the end of this lesson, students will be able to:

  • Explain what to do and say during a police encounter, including their Miranda rights
  • Identify when to contact a lawyer and where to find free or low-cost legal help
  • Describe basic tenant rights: leases, heat, security deposits, and how to report problems
  • Match common needs to the right government office (City Hall, RMV, SSA, USCIS)
  • Use the 6 key vocabulary words accurately in context
  • Compare life in the U.S. with their home country in speaking and writing

Step-by-Step Procedure

1. Warm-up & Vocabulary Preview 2:00–2:20 Slides 1–2 · Worksheet Ex. 1

Purpose: Introduce the three topics and pre-teach the key words before the input.

  • Show Slide 1. Ask: "What do you think 'know your rights' means?" Take 2–3 answers.
  • Show Slide 2 and briefly introduce the three topics.
  • Students do Exercise 1 (Vocabulary Matching) alone (5 min), check with a partner (3 min), then click Check Exercise 1.
  • Review quickly. Model pronunciation: Mi-RAN-da, na-tu-ra-li-ZA-tion, se-CUR-i-ty de-POS-it.

2. Part One: Police Encounters 2:20–2:45 Slides 3–6

Purpose: Give students simple, practical steps and phrases they can use if police stop them.

  • Slide 4: Drill the two key phrases chorally:
    "Am I being detained, or am I free to leave?" and "I am going to remain silent. I would like to speak to a lawyer."
  • Slide 5: Read the Miranda warning aloud. Check understanding: "Do police have to read this when they just ask for your ID?" (No: only in custody AND being questioned.)
  • Slide 6: Ask: "Who would you call if you couldn't pay a lawyer?" Point out the difference between civil help (legal aid) and criminal cases (public defender).
Sensitivity note: Some students may have stressful experiences with police or immigration. Keep the conversation general. Make it clear that students can talk about "someone they know" or a situation they have seen, and never ask anyone to share their own legal history. This lesson gives general information, not legal advice. Encourage students with a specific problem to contact a legal aid office.

3. Part One Activation 2:45–3:15 Slide 7 · Worksheet Ex. 2

  • A. Conversation (10 min): Pairs discuss the guiding question, then each student writes one thing they learned from their partner.
  • B. Share Your Opinion (8 min): Students write their most important tip. Take 4–5 quick shares, then vote on the class's top tip.
  • C. Writing (10 min): Students write 3–5 sentences. Model one sentence on the board using "I would…" plus a quote.
  • Students click Check Exercise 2 to compare with the sample answer (2 min).

4. Part Two: Leases, Heat & Deposits 3:15–3:40 Slides 8–10

  • Slide 9: Ask: "What would you check first before signing a lease?" Teach utilities and verbal promise.
  • Slide 10: Convert temperatures for students who think in Celsius: 64°F ≈ 18°C, 68°F ≈ 20°C. Explain that the rules depend on your city and state, so tenants should check their local law.
  • Concept check: "Your landlord kept $300 of your deposit. What must they give you?" (An itemized list of reasons.)
  • Before the break, show Slide 11.

Break 3:40–4:10 Slide 11

5. Part Two: Reporting Violations 4:10–4:20 Slide 12

  • Quick recap of the first half: "Name one thing from before the break."
  • Walk through the 4 steps. Ask students to put them in order from memory with a partner (1 min).

6. Part Two Activation 4:20–4:50 Slide 13 · Worksheet Ex. 3

  • A. Conversation (8 min): Pairs compare landlord-tenant relationships, then write one difference.
  • B. Opinion (7 min): Students choose heat, deposit, or lease terms and give a reason. Do a quick hands-up count for each.
  • C. Writing (13 min): Students write a comparison paragraph (at least 4 sentences). Board the comparison phrases: In the U.S., … but in my home country, …; However, …; Both … and …
  • Students click Check Exercise 3 (2 min).

7. Part Three: Government Offices 4:50–5:10 Slides 14–17

  • Slides 15–16: Explain local and state (City Hall, RMV) vs. federal (SSA, USCIS). Note that RMV is the Massachusetts name; most states say DMV.
  • Slide 17 quick quiz: Call out a need and have students answer with the office: "I need a marriage license." → City Hall; "I lost my Social Security card." → SSA; "I want to renew my license." → RMV; "I want a work permit." → USCIS.

8. Part Three Activation 5:10–5:35 Slide 18 · Worksheet Ex. 4

  • A. Conversation (7 min) and B. Opinion (5 min) as before.
  • C. Writing (11 min): Students write step-by-step instructions, one step per line. Board the sequence words: First, Next, Then, After that, Finally. Remind them to use the past tense.
  • Students click Check Exercise 4 (2 min).

9. Vocabulary Review, Let's Discuss & Close 5:35–5:50 Slides 19–21 · Worksheet Ex. 5

  • Slide 19: Read the six words together.
  • Exercise 5 (6 min): Students fill the blanks, then click Check Exercise 5.
  • Slide 20 (6 min): Pairs ask and answer the five questions. Take one answer per question.
  • Slide 21: Close and take questions. Remind students to save their worksheet as a PDF if you collect it.

Timing Tips

  • Running behind? Assign Part C writing for Exercises 3 and 4 as homework. Students' answers stay saved on the same device.
  • Ahead of schedule? Role-play a police stop or a call to a landlord about broken heat, using the phrases from Slides 4 and 12.

Common Student Mistakes

  • "Lease" vs. "rent": the lease is the contract, and rent is the money you pay each month.
  • "Security deposit" vs. "first month's rent": the deposit is returned when you move out, minus any damage costs.
  • Naturalization vs. a green card: a green card means permanent residency, and naturalization means becoming a citizen.
  • "I would" + base verb: students may write "I would said" or "I would to say". Correct it to "I would say".
  • Prepositions: apply for naturalization, call a lawyer (not "call to a lawyer"), sign a lease.

Exercises 2–4 — Open-ended

Answers will vary. Look for:

  • 2C: 3–5 sentences, "I would…" conditional, at least one tip from Slide 4 (stay calm, show ID when driving, ask if free to leave, remain silent / ask for a lawyer).
  • 3C: a paragraph of at least 4 sentences with comparison language and at least one fact from Slides 9–12 (lease, heat, deposit, reporting).
  • 4C: at least 3 clear steps in time order, with sequence words and past-tense verbs, that name the correct office.

Let's Discuss (Slide 20) — Suggested Answers

  1. Stay calm and polite, keep your hands visible, and ask: "Am I being detained, or am I free to leave?"
  2. The rent and due date, the lease length, which utilities are included, and the rules about guests or pets.
  3. Tell the landlord in writing and keep a copy. If it is not fixed, contact code enforcement or the health department.
  4. The RMV (or DMV in most states).
  5. A legal aid office, the Public Defender's Office (for criminal cases), a local bar association referral service, or an immigrant legal services nonprofit.
