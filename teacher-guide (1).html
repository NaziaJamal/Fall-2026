Learning objectives

By the end of the lesson, students will be able to:

  • Explain at least 4 American values (freedom, equality, independence, hard work) with an example.
  • Describe at least 5 everyday customs: greetings, small talk, tipping, punctuality, personal space, taboo topics.
  • Match 5 major U.S. holidays to their month and traditions, and read a simple American menu.
  • Use the 18 target words in speaking and writing.
  • Compare U.S. culture to their own using sentence frames.
  • Perform a short role-play in an everyday American situation.

Stage-by-Stage

0:00–0:05

Stage 1 · Welcome

Slides 1–2 · Whole class
Teacher
  1. Greet students at the door. Show slide 1: device users scan the QR code to open the worksheet; paper users take a printed copy.
  2. Show slide 2 and walk through the three themes.
“Today we start our journey into American culture — what Americans believe, how they behave, and what they eat and celebrate. By the end of today, you'll know 18 new words and you'll have a real conversation about American culture.”

Everyone has a worksheet open or printed.

0:05–0:25

Stage 2 · Culture Bingo

Slide 3 · Mingle · 15-min timer
Teacher
  1. 1 minShow slide 3. Phone users scan the QR code; paper users turn to the Icebreaker page.
  2. 1 minModel one exchange with a student:
    “Have you ever experienced culture shock in the U.S.?” — “Yes.” — “Really? What happened?”
    Point out that a follow-up question is required before writing a name.
  3. 15 minStart the slide timer. Circulate. Encourage quiet students; make sure follow-up questions are happening. When someone shouts BINGO, check one of their squares and let everyone keep playing.
  4. 3 minDebrief: 2–3 winners share one interesting thing they learned about a classmate.
Transition: “You just practiced small talk — something Americans do every day. Let's talk about WHY Americans act this way: their values.”
Students

Ask questions, follow up, collect a different name for each square.

Expected

Each student talks to 8–12 classmates. Energy is high.

Running late? Stop at 12 minutes and skip the debrief. The 25-square card is hard to fill, so don't wait for a full card.
0:25–0:50

Stage 3 · American Values & Beliefs

Slides 4–6 · Worksheet 1A–1B · Pairs
Teacher
  1. 3 minSlide 4 warm-up (timer 3:00). Pairs answer the question. Collect 4–6 ideas in the VALUES column.
  2. 7 minSlide 5 vocabulary. Say each word; students repeat. Ask one concept-check question per word, for example:
    • Independence: “If your parents pay for everything, are you independent?” (No)
    • Ambition: “Is ambition about wanting to succeed, or wanting to relax?” (Succeed)
    • Patriotism: “Is flying your country's flag an example of patriotism?” (Yes)
    Students write their own sentence for at least 3 words (1A).
  3. 8 minSlide 6 reading (timer 8:00). Pairs read and answer questions 1–3. Faster pairs do the Challenge question 4.
  4. 5 minCheck: Q2 = Humor. 2–3 pairs share Q3. Ask one strong pair for Q4.
  5. 2 minBuffer.
Transition: “Values are ideas. Customs are how those ideas show up in everyday behavior.”
Students

Discuss, write 3 sentences, read and answer.

Expected

10+ ideas on the board; 1A has at least 3 personal sentences; 1B answered.

Watch for: “All Americans…” Ask: “Is that true everywhere in the U.S.?” The reading's last line is there for this.

Stage 4 · Social Customs

Slides 7–10 · Worksheet 2A–2C · Groups → pairs
Teacher

  2. 6 minSlide 8 vocabulary. Teach custom vs. tradition here (see Common mistakes). Students write 3 sentences (2A).
  3. 8 minSlide 9 True or False. Read each statement. Students stand (true) or stay seated (false). Ask one student why, then press → to reveal the answer. Students mark T/F and a short note (2B).
  4. 7 minSlide 10 dialogue (timer 7:00). Pairs read it twice and switch roles. Then they answer 2C. Check quickly using the answer key.

“Break time! Try some small talk in English with someone from another table.”
Students

Share stories, play True/False, read the dialogue.

Expected

All 6 custom words understood; 2B and 2C complete.

Statement 3 has no single answer. Use it to discuss how customs change between big cities and small towns.

Answer Keys

1B · Reading

1Any person, from anywhere, can succeed if they work hard enough.
2Humor is not in the reading. Freedom, hard work, equality, and independence are.
3–4Open answers. For Q4, good answers give a reason. Accept both yes and no.

2B · True or False

1False. “How are you?” is a greeting. The expected answer is short: “Good, thanks! And you?”
2False. Punctuality matters at work. “On time” often means 5 minutes early.
3It depends. Common in small towns and suburbs, less common in big cities.
4False. A 15–20% tip is expected at sit-down restaurants. It pays for the service; servers depend on it.
5False. Salary is taboo in casual conversation.

2C · Dialogue

1Any two: a lunch tradition on someone's first day · splitting the bill · leaving about a 20% tip · being on time (“9 sharp”).
2“9 sharp” = exactly at 9:00. “You in?” = Do you want to join us?
3Open answer.

3B · Menu question 5

$13.99 + $6.50 = $20.49 · 20% tip = $4.10 (rounded) · total = $24.59 (no tax).

Sentence Frames, Support & Common Mistakes

Describing a customIn America, it is common to ______.
Comparing culturesIn my country, we ______, but in the U.S., people ______.
Expressing surpriseI was surprised to learn that ______.
Asking about customsIs it normal in the U.S. to ______?
Giving an opinionI think the most interesting American custom is ______.
Talking about foodI have / have never tried ______. I would like to ______.
AgreeingI agree. In my experience, ______.
Disagreeing politelyI see your point, but in my culture, ______.
Support (lower A2+)
  • Students keep the sentence frames (worksheet reference page) open all lesson.
  • Ask for 2 personal sentences per vocabulary set instead of 3.
  • Seat stronger and weaker speakers together for pair work.
  • Role-play: allow a short written script.
Stretch (stronger B1)
  • Challenge questions: 1B Q4 and Section 4 Q5.
  • “Which American values do you agree with? Which seem strange?”
  • “When could a U.S. custom cause a misunderstanding with someone from your country?”
  • Write a 5-sentence paragraph using 5 words from any set.

Common mistakes

  • Custom vs. tradition. A custom is how people usually behave in a society (tipping, shaking hands). A tradition is something a family or group does again and again, often for a special day, and passes down (turkey on Thanksgiving).
  • “All Americans…” Redirect: “Is that true in every part of the U.S.? Is there diversity?”
  • Taboo ≠ illegal. Taboo means impolite or uncomfortable to talk about, not against the law.
  • Small talk translation. Students may translate greetings from their first language. Model real patterns: “How's it going?” — “Pretty good, you?”

What Changed from Version 3

  • One schedule everywhere. Slides, guide, and worksheet use the same timeline. The video now has its own 20-minute stage; the break is 15 minutes; the three topic stages are 25 minutes each.
  • Matching numbers. All slide numbers in this guide match the 18-slide deck, and the worksheet sections (Icebreaker, 1–6) match both.
  • One Bingo card. The 25-square card, on phones (index.html) or on paper (printed worksheet).
  • Removed: the card sort (its cards were never made, and it overlapped True/False). The vocabulary matching game moved to Day 2 as a warm-up (cards above).
  • Less copying. Definitions and examples are printed on the worksheet. Students write their own sentences for 3 words per set.
  • Six role-play scenarios (A–F) for six groups of five, the same on the slide, cards, and worksheet.
  • Content fixes:
    • Reading Q2 now asks which value is not in the text (four of the five options were in it).
    • “The Constitution promises equality” became a quote from the Declaration of Independence.
    • Personal space: “about an arm's length” instead of “2 feet.”
    • Small talk: “people you don't know well.”
    • Taboo: “impolite or uncomfortable” instead of “offensive.”
    • Custom and tradition no longer define each other.
    • True/False #3 is now “It depends.”
    • Menu #5 says “no tax” and has an answer.
    • The holiday matching uses letters and months, so it works on paper and on devices.
    • The reading adds a line that not all Americans think the same way.
  • Video: the title and link are now set by you in CONFIG, so the slide never shows a video that doesn't exist.
