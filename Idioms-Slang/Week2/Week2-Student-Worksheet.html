Part 1 Lina's Busy Saturday

Read the story. Find the 6 phrasal verbs about moving and going places. Write them in order.

It's Saturday morning. Lina's sister texts her: "Can you pick up Mom at the train station at 10? Her train gets in at 10:05." Lina replies: "Sure! 👍"

First, Lina drops off her son, Omar, at soccer practice. Then she heads out to the station. Ten minutes later, she checks her bag. No phone! She turns around and drives back home to get it.

At the station, Mom's train is late, so Lina kills time at the coffee cart. And guess who she runs into? Dev, her old coworker! They make small talk about the weather and the Red Sox.

At 10:40, Mom finally arrives. Lina texts Omar's coach: "So sorry, running late! I'll come back for Omar at 12:15."

👀 Slang to recognize (not to use): bounce and dip can mean "leave" in very casual American English: "I'm gonna bounce." "Nobody was there, so we dipped." Understand them when you hear them. For you, head out is the safe, natural choice almost everywhere.

Part 9 Tell Your Partner

Ask and answer. Use at least 2 phrasal verbs in each answer. Then ask one follow-up question. You can talk about yourself or invent a character.

  1. Who or what do you sometimes pick up or drop off? (a person, a package, food, dry cleaning…)
  2. When did you last run into someone you know? Where were you? Did you make small talk?
  3. What time do you usually head out in the morning? What do you always do before you head out?
  4. Have you ever had to turn around because you forgot something? What was it?
  5. B1 Think of a place you love to come back to (a café, a park, a city). Why do you keep coming back?
Helpful follow-ups: Oh really? Where? · What happened next? · Was that hard? · No way! · How about you?
