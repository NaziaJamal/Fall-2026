This Week's Focus

Targetsgive up · start over · come up with · figure out · work out · sort out
GrammarSeparable vs. inseparable. With a pronoun, the verb must split: figure it out, not figure out it. Come up with never splits. Give up + -ing.
SLOApply the separable/inseparable pattern (A2+ SLO 3); explain that phrasal-verb meaning is not literal (SLO 2).
RecycleWk 1: break the ice · make small talk · shoot the breeze/chat · kill time · hit it off
Wk 2: pick up · drop off · turn around · run into · head out · come back
OutcomeStudents describe a problem and give advice in a 2–3 min conversation using 2–3 targets with correct pronoun position.

Why This Week Matters

5 of the 15 Final core expressions are taught today: figure out, come up with, give up, sort out, start over.

Midterm (Week 6): speaking cards #3, #6 and #9 depend on these verbs. Today's Conversation Missions are the first rehearsal of the midterm format (2–3 min, 3 expressions, a card).

Register check (ongoing): during Missions, note pronoun-position errors and register choices on your clipboard for feedback at the end.

⚠ Two Teaching Notes Before You Start

"work out" in American English. Oxford labels work out = "solve / understand" as especially British. Americans default to figure out for that. Teach work out with its four American uses: exercise ("I work out"), end well ("It'll work out"), plan details ("work out the schedule"), resolve between people ("You'll work it out"). Don't present it as a synonym of figure out.
"sort out" + slang. Natural and current in the U.S. (Merriam-Webster shows 2025 U.S. news use), though Americans also say deal with / straighten out. "Sorted!" alone = British casual → recognition only. U.S. equivalent: "All set!" / "It's fixed."   start over is labeled "chiefly US" — British say start again.

B · Engage: "Dana's Terrible Week"Class0:15–0:25

Tell the story day by day (draw simple icons on the board). Before revealing each phrase, ask students to predict: "What do you think she did?" Write the target phrase only after they guess the idea.

Story (tell it)Reveal
Monday: Dana's Wi-Fi stops. She restarts the computer. Nothing. She restarts her phone. Nothing. Then she sees it — the router is unplugged!She figured it out.
Tuesday: Her phone bill is $60. Usually it's $30. She calls the company. Twenty minutes later, the bill is correct.She sorted it out.
Wednesday: Her bus is cancelled. She thinks… and asks her neighbor for a ride.She came up with a plan.
Thursday: She's on page 4 of a 5-page online application. The website crashes. Everything is gone.She had to start over.
She's tired. She wants to stop. Class poll: "Hands up — would YOU stop or try again?" Then: Dana tries again and finishes.She didn't give up.

Class discussion (2 min): "Which day was the worst for Dana? Have you had a day like that?" (Optional — students can just choose a day.)

C · New Expressions in ContextClass → Pairs0:25–0:55
  1. Meaning check (10 min). Use Dana's story. For each verb ask a concept-check question (CCQ) — see table on next page. Students then open the Toolkit (worksheet p. 1) and read the examples with a partner.
  2. Grammar discovery (8 min). Write on the board: She figured out the problem. / She figured the problem out. / She figured it out. / She figured out it. Ask: "Three are correct. Which one is wrong? Why?" Elicit the rule: pronouns go in the middle. Then: She came up with it / She came up it with → "Does come up with split?" (No — never.) Show the Pronoun Rule box. Add: give up + -ing → "He gave up smoking," not "gave up to smoke."
  3. Pronunciation (6 min). Back-chain: OUT → DOUT → i-DOUT → figyer-i-DOUT. Then sor-di-DOUT, gi-vi-DUP, wor-ki-DOUT. Stress is on the particle: figure OUT, give UP, come UP with. Pairs whisper each phrase to each other, then say it in a sentence: "Don't worry — we'll figure it out."
  4. Register (3 min). Ask: "Can you say 'figure it out' to your boss? To a teacher? In a job interview?" (Yes — all are neutral and safe.) "In a very formal letter?" (Better: determine / resolve.) Preview Ex 6.
  5. Pairs: personalize (3 min). "Tell your partner one thing you figured out this week — even something small."

D · ListeningPairs / Groups0:55–1:15

Part 1 · VOA (8 min). Before: pairs predict the order of 4 steps (worksheet). During: play once; students check order and complete Q2–4. After: groups apply the 4 steps to Kevin's problem (bridges into Part 2). Why this source: VOA Learning English is a credible, U.S. learner broadcaster at slower speed — ideal for A2+; it shows figure out and work out (= plan carefully) used by real American journalists at work.

Part 2 · "No Heat in October" (12 min). Before: class question (worksheet). Listen 1: True/False. Listen 2: "Catch the pronoun" — the point is noticing it in the middle. After: pairs advise Kevin. Share the Massachusetts heating fact (Boston.gov). Read with a confident student at natural speed; slow down only for Listen 2.

Rosa:Morning! You look tired.
Kevin:Ugh, I am. The heat in my apartment isn't working, and it was like forty degrees last night.
Rosa:In October? Did you call your landlord?
Kevin:Three times. No answer. I almost gave up and bought a space heater.
Rosa:Okay, don't give up yet. Did you check the thermostat? Sometimes it's just the batteries.
Kevin:I tried. I couldn't figure it out. The screen just says "Error 4."
Rosa:Hmm. My building had the same problem last year. We called the management company directly, not the landlord. They sorted it out in one day.
Kevin:Really? I didn't know that was an option.
Rosa:Yeah. And write everything down — dates, times, who you talked to.
Kevin:That's smart. I need to come up with a plan, because I'm not sleeping in my coat again.
Rosa:Ha! You'll work it out. Want to grab a coffee before the meeting?
Kevin:Definitely. Then I'll call them on my lunch break.

~140 words · 50–60 seconds · targets: gave up, give up, figure it out, sorted it out, come up with, work it out. "like forty degrees" = about 40°F (casual filler "like" — point it out for recognition).

E · Controlled Practice 1Solo → Pairs1:15–1:40
  • Ex 1 (6 min, solo → check with partner). Same stories as context — students now produce form (past tense + pronoun position).
  • Ex 2 Pronoun Swap (5 min, pairs). Partners take turns: one reads the original aloud, the other says the swap before writing. Item 5 is the trap.
  • Ex 3 work out (5 min, pairs). Then ask: "Which meaning do you hear most?" (exercise, end well).
  • Check together (2 min). Project answers; drill the 2–3 most-missed items aloud.
  • Ex 7 Talk to Your Partner (7 min). Pair with someone new. Each answer must include a follow-up question. Students choose real-life or "or" options.
A2+: keep the Toolkit open; answer Ex 7 Q1 and Q4 only, using the frames.   B1: close the Toolkit for Ex 2; in Ex 7, answer all four and use two different verbs per answer.

G · Register: "Would You Say This?"Pairs → Class0:18–0:28

Ex 6 (5 min, pairs), then the stand-up game (5 min, class): read each line; students stand if it's natural for the situation, stay seated if not. Ask one seated student to fix it.

Line (read aloud)Natural?
1. To your boss: "I figured out what was wrong with the printer."✔ Stand
2. In a job interview: "I'm super good at sorting stuff out, lol."✘ → "I'm good at sorting out problems."
3. Texting a friend: "Don't give up!! You got this 💪"✔ Stand ("You got this" = casual, very common)
4. A formal email to a college office: "Can you guys figure it out?"✘ → "Could you please help me resolve this?"
5. To a neighbor: "Did you work out the parking thing with the landlord?"✔ Stand
6. To your American teacher, after fixing a problem: "Sorted!"✘ (British) → "All set!" / "I figured it out!"

Key message: These six verbs are safe almost everywhere. What changes the register is the words around them (lol, stuff, you guys) and very formal writing.

H · Dialogue RemixPairs0:28–0:40
  1. Assign by level: A or B (A2+), C or D (B1). Mixed pairs choose B.
  2. Read twice, switching roles (4 min). Underline the verbs.
  3. Remix (5 min): change 3 details, then perform without the paper. It's okay to change the words — the goal is natural conversation, not memorization.
  4. 2–3 pairs perform for the class (3 min). Class listens for: "Which verbs did you hear?"
I · The Fix-It AgencyPairs → Groups → Class0:40–1:10
  1. Set-up (2 min). "You work at the Fix-It Agency. People bring you problems. Your job: figure out the real problem, come up with solutions, and decide — give up, start over, or keep going?"
  2. Round 1 · Client role-plays (8 min, pairs). Each pair takes a Client Card. A = client, B = agent (3 min). Swap cards with the next pair and swap roles (3 min). Monitor and note good language.
  3. Round 2 · Case File (8 min, groups of 3–4). Two pairs join. Choose one of their clients. Assign roles (Manager, Writer, Speaker, Language Checker). Complete the Case File, including one Worst Advice.
  4. Pitches (8 min, class). Each Speaker gives a 1-minute pitch with the frames. Listeners write the best idea on a sticky note.
  5. Class vote + feedback (4 min). Best Solution · Worst Advice Award · Most Natural English. Give 2–3 language points from your notes (praise first).
A2+: take the Client role first (describing a problem is easier than advising); use the Useful Language box.   B1: Agent first; Card 5 Round 2 — play the customer-service rep; Card 8 — use work out with two meanings; as Speaker, paraphrase instead of reading the frames.
Pairing: put an A2+ client with a B1 agent in Round 1, then swap — the B1 student models, the A2+ student then uses the same language.
J · Conversation Missions (spontaneous)Pairs, rotating1:10–1:22
  1. Round 1 (5 min). Pairs draw a Mission card. 1 minute to think — no writing. Talk 2–3 minutes. Use at least 2 expressions.
  2. Rotate. One line of students moves one seat. New partner, new card.
  3. Round 2 (5 min). No thinking time, no notes, cards face-down after reading. Try 3 expressions. The second round should feel easier — tell them that's the goal.
  4. Debrief (2 min). "Which expression came out naturally — without thinking?"
Your observation log (register check + midterm prep): walk around and tally — ✔ correct pronoun position · ✘ "figure out it"-type errors · ✔ natural recycling of Week 1–2 expressions · register slips. Share 2 anonymous errors at the end of class ("I heard… What's better?").
K · Agree or Disagree? (Ex 8)Groups → Class1:22–1:30

Groups of 3–4 discuss as many statements as they can (5 min). Every person gives one reason. Each group's Reporter shares one group opinion with the class (3 min). Push follow-up: "Does everyone agree? Who disagrees — why?" Statement 1 ("never give up") usually starts the best discussion — sometimes giving up is the right choice.

L · Quick Write & Wrap-UpSolo → Class1:30–1:40
  • Ex 9 Quick Write (4 min). Reply to the friend's text. Remind: "Two verbs, naturally. Don't force five!" (Syllabus: one correct expression is worth more than five awkward ones.)
  • One True Sentence (2 min). 4–5 volunteers read theirs. Class question: "Which expression would you actually use outside class this week?"
  • Exit Ticket (3 min) + assign the Phrase Journal (1 min): 3 expressions heard or used outside class; bring it to Week 4.

Personal Topics · Always Optional

All prompts have a neutral path: Ex 7 offers "or" options; every Client Card is a neutral adult problem (heat, package, portal, bus, bill, teammate, driving test, hobby); students may invent a character on any card. No card asks about immigration, health, family hardship or employment status. Earlier Week 3 materials had a Massachusetts-specific personal quick write — this version keeps the topic choice with the student ("you choose").

Language Notes & Corrections to Earlier Week 3 Materials

  • Old Ex 2 #6 ("The city had to ___ with a completely new plan") produced come up with with. Not reused.
  • Old Ex 6 #3 ("Don't give ( up it / it up / up )") had two correct answers: "Don't give it up" and "Don't give up." Not reused.
  • Old grammar note listed start it over as a model. It's possible ("start her life over") but uncommon; this version teaches start over without an object.
  • Old Ex 1 defined work out as "find a solution." In American English, teach figure out for that, and work out for exercise / end well / plan / resolve between people.
  • The spring/Boston Marathon context has been replaced with October contexts (heat, bus schedules, bills).
