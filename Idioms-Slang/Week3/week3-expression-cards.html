EXPRESSION 1 / 6🧩

📖 The situation

Maya's laptop won't turn on. She checks the battery. She restarts it. Then she sees it: the cable is loose! “Aha! I figured it out!”

figure out

🤔 What do you think figure it out means? Talk with your partner. Then tap Reveal.

✅ Meaning

to understand something, or find the answer, usually after thinking about it

Source: Merriam-Webster: “discover, determine; solve”

💬 Examples

  • I can't figure out this new app.
  • Don't worry. We'll figure it out.
  • Can you figure out how to turn on the heat?

🧩 How to use it

  • ✔ figure out the problem
  • ✔ figure the problem out
  • ✔ figure it out
  • ✘ figure out it
  • ✔ figure out how / why / what…: I can't figure out how to log in.

✘ I finally figured out it.
✔ I finally figured it out.

🇺🇸 For “solve / understand,” Americans say figure out much more often than work out.

🗣️ Mini-dialogue

A: Is the printer working now?
B: Yeah, I finally figured it out.
A: Really? What was wrong?
B: It was out of paper! Ha ha.

🎵 Say it naturally

figure it OUT → “figyer-i-DOUT”

Stress the small word. Link the words together. In American English, a t between vowels sounds like a soft d.

💼 a coworker✔ Yes
🏠 a neighbor or stranger✔ Yes
🎓 your teacher✔ Yes
🤝 a job interview✔ Yes“I like to figure out problems.”

✍️ Formal writing → determine

⭐ B1 challenge: more meanings

  • figure someone out = understand a person's behavior: “I can't figure him out. Is he angry or just quiet?”
  • I figured… (informal) = I thought / I guessed: “I figured you'd be hungry, so I brought pizza.”

EXPRESSION 2 / 6💡

📖 The situation

It's raining. The kids are bored. Omar thinks for a minute… then he has an idea: a picnic on the living room floor! Omar came up with a great idea.

come up with

🤔 What do you think come up with means? Talk with your partner. Then tap Reveal.

✅ Meaning

to think of an idea, a plan, or a solution, especially for a problem

Source: Merriam-Webster: “to produce especially in dealing with a problem or challenge”

💬 Examples

  • We came up with a plan to share the driving.
  • She always comes up with great names for things.
  • Can you come up with a better solution?

🧩 How to use it

  • ✔ come up with an idea / a plan / a solution / a name
  • ✔ come up with it
  • ✘ come it up with
  • ✘ come up a plan (don't forget with!)

✘ I came up a good plan.
✔ I came up with a good plan.

This is a three-part verb. It never splits, and you always need with. Past tense: came up with.

🗣️ Mini-dialogue

A: How are we getting to the concert? There's no bus at night.
B: Don't worry. I came up with a plan.
A: Oh yeah? What is it?
B: My cousin can drive us. We'll just buy him dinner!

🎵 Say it naturally

come UP with → “kuh-MUP-with”

Stress the small word. Link the words together. In American English, a t between vowels sounds like a soft d.

🎯 Would you say it to…?

🧑‍🤝‍🧑 a close friend✔ Yes
💼 a coworker✔ Yes
🏠 a neighbor or stranger✔ Yes
🎓 your teacher✔ Yes
🤝 a job interview✔ Yes“I came up with a new schedule.”

✍️ Formal writing → develop / create

⭐ B1 challenge: more meanings

  • come up (no with) = be mentioned: “Your name came up in the meeting.”
  • Something came up. = something unexpected happened: “Sorry, something came up. I can't come tonight.”

🎤 Your turn (pairs)

Your friend's birthday is next week, and you have only $20. With your partner, come up with a party plan.

EXPRESSION 3 / 6🏳️

📖 The situation

Ana took guitar lessons for one month. Her fingers hurt. “It's too hard,” she said, and she stopped. Ana gave up.

give up

🤔 What do you think give up means? Talk with your partner. Then tap Reveal.

✅ Meaning

(1) to stop trying, to quit · (2) to stop doing or having something, like a habit

Source: Merriam-Webster: “to cease doing or attempting something… quit”

💬 Examples

  • Don't give up! You're almost there.
  • I gave up sugar for a month.
  • He gave up smoking last year.

🧩 How to use it

  • ✔ Don't give up! (no object)
  • ✔ give up coffee / give it up
  • ✘ give up it
  • ✔ give up + -ing: give up smoking
  • ✘ give up to smoke
  • ✔ give up on + someone/something

✘ He gave up to smoke.
✔ He gave up smoking.

“Don't give up!” is one of the most common ways to encourage someone in English.

🗣️ Mini-dialogue

A: Ugh. I failed my driving test again.
B: Oh no! But don't give up.
A: I almost gave up on driving. Maybe I'll just take the bus forever.
B: No way. Third time's the charm!

🎵 Say it naturally

give UP · give it UP → “gi-VUP · gi-vi-DUP”

Stress the small word. Link the words together. In American English, a t between vowels sounds like a soft d.

🎯 Would you say it to…?

💼 a coworker✔ Yes
🏠 a neighbor or stranger✔ Yes
🎓 your teacher✔ Yes
🤝 a job interview✔ Yes“I don't give up easily.”

✍️ Formal writing → quit / stop

⭐ B1 challenge: more meanings

EXPRESSION 4 / 6🔄

📖 The situation

Carlos is on page 4 of a long online form. Suddenly, the page freezes. Everything is gone! Carlos has to start over.

start over

🤔 What do you think start over means? Talk with your partner. Then tap Reveal.

✅ Meaning

to begin again from the beginning

Source: Merriam-Webster: “to begin doing something again” · labeled chiefly US

💬 Examples

  • The website crashed, so I had to start over.
  • I burned the rice, so I started over.
  • Moving to a new city can be a chance to start over.

🧩 How to use it

  • ✔ I had to start over. (usually no object)
  • ✔ start over from scratch = from nothing
  • ✔ start all over again (very common in speech)

✘ I started over my computer.
✔ I restarted my computer.

🇺🇸 Americans say start over. British speakers usually say start again. For phones and computers, say restart.

🗣️ Mini-dialogue

A: Is your essay finished?
B: No! My laptop died, and I didn't save it.
A: Oh no. So you have to start over?
B: Yep. From scratch.

🎵 Say it naturally

start OVER → “star-DOH-ver”

Stress the small word. Link the words together. In American English, a t between vowels sounds like a soft d.

🎯 Would you say it to…?

🧑‍🤝‍🧑 a close friend✔ Yes
💼 a coworker✔ Yes
🏠 a neighbor or stranger✔ Yes
🎓 your teacher✔ Yes
🤝 a job interview✔ Yes“When a plan fails, I'm not afraid to start over.”

✍️ Formal writing → begin again

⭐ B1 challenge: more meanings

EXPRESSION 5 / 6🗂️

📖 The situation

Linh's internet bill is $40 too high. She calls the company. Ten minutes later, the bill is correct. “Great, they sorted it out!”

sort out

🤔 What do you think sort it out means? Talk with your partner. Then tap Reveal.

✅ Meaning

to fix or deal with a problem; to organize something messy

Source: Merriam-Webster; Oxford Learner's Dictionaries

💬 Examples

  • I spent an hour on the phone to sort out my bill.
  • Let me talk to the manager. I'll sort it out.
  • I need to sort out my closet this weekend.

🧩 How to use it

  • ✔ sort out the problem
  • ✔ sort the problem out
  • ✔ sort it out
  • ✘ sort out it
  • ✔ It'll sort itself out. = The problem will go away by itself.

✘ We need to sort out it before Friday.
✔ We need to sort it out before Friday.

🇺🇸 Americans use sort out, and also deal with and straighten out. 👀 “Sorted!” alone is British slang: recognize it only.

🗣️ Mini-dialogue

A: Hi, I'm calling about my phone bill. I was charged twice.
B: I'm sorry about that. Let me see if I can sort that out for you.
A: Thank you.
B: Okay, you're all set. You'll get a refund in three to five days.

🎵 Say it naturally

SORT it OUT → “sor-di-DOUT”

Stress the small word. Link the words together. In American English, a t between vowels sounds like a soft d.

🎯 Would you say it to…?

🧑‍🤝‍🧑 a close friend✔ Yes
💼 a coworker✔ Yes
🏠 a neighbor or stranger✔ Yes
🎓 your teacher✔ Yes
🤝 a job interview✔ Yes“I'm good at sorting out problems.”

EXPRESSION 6 / 6💪

📖 The situation

🏋️ Dee goes to the gym every morning. She works out.
🤝 Ravi and his roommate argued, then talked and made a cleaning schedule. They worked it out.
🎉 Dana's week was terrible, but by Friday everything was fine. Everything worked out.

work out

🤔 Same verb, three stories. Is the meaning the same each time? Talk with your partner. Then tap Reveal.

✅ Meaning

(1) to exercise · (2) to end well, have a good result · (3) to plan the details · (4) to fix a problem between people

Source: Oxford Advanced American Dictionary; Oxford Learner's Dictionaries

💬 Examples

  • I work out three times a week.
  • Don't worry. It'll all work out.
  • We still need to work out the details.

🧩 How to use it

  • ✔ I work out. / It worked out. (no object)
  • ✔ work out the details / work it out
  • ✘ work out it
  • ✔ noun: a workout (one word)

✘ We had a fight, but we worked out it.
✔ We had a fight, but we worked it out.

🇺🇸 For “solve / understand,” Americans usually say figure out: “I can't figure out this math problem.” Work out a problem sounds more British.

🗣️ Mini-dialogue

A: How's the new job?
B: Good! I was nervous at first, but it's working out.
A: Nice! Are you still working out every morning?
B: Ha! No. I'm too tired!

🎵 Say it naturally

WORK OUT · work it OUT → “wor-KOUT · wor-ki-DOUT”

Stress the small word. Link the words together. In American English, a t between vowels sounds like a soft d.

🎯 Would you say it to…?

🧑‍🤝‍🧑 a close friend✔ Yes
💼 a coworker✔ Yes
🏠 a neighbor or stranger✔ Yes
🎓 your teacher✔ Yes
🤝 a job interview✔ Yes“It all worked out well.”

✍️ Formal writing → exercise / succeed / resolve

⭐ B1 challenge: more meanings

  • work out to = come to a total: “The bill works out to about $20 each.”
  • It didn't work out. = it ended, gently (a job, an apartment, a relationship): “The apartment didn't work out, so I moved.”

🎤 Your turn (pairs)

Do you work out? Or: Tell your partner about a plan that didn't work out, but something good happened anyway.

Personal stories are always optional. You can invent a character.

SLANG SPOTTER👀

📖 The situation

You're at work. The printer is broken. Your British coworker fixes it, smiles, and says: “Sorted!”

“Sorted!”

🤔 What do you think Sorted! means?

✅ Meaning

It's fixed. / It's done. / Everything is OK.

Source: Oxford Learner's Dictionaries: sort out, British English, informal

🚦 Recognize it, don't use it

👀 Recognize It's British and casual. You'll hear it on British TV and from British coworkers.

In the U.S., say:

  • All set!
  • It's fixed!
  • Done!

🎯 Would you say it to…?

🧑‍🤝‍🧑 a close friend🤔 MaybeOnly if you're in the U.K., or joking
💼 a coworker🤔 MaybeAmericans may not use it
🎓 your teacher✘ No“I figured it out!” / “All set!”
🤝 a job interview✘ No

Knowing an expression doesn't mean you have to use it. 🙂
