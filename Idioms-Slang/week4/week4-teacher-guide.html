This Week's Focus

Targetsgrow apart · get along (with) · stand someone up · get on well (with) · patch things up
Grammarstand someone up: the person goes in the middle (stood me up, not stood up me); passive got stood up. get along / patch things up + with; grow apart + optional from.
SLOUse stand someone up in active and passive form; choose between get along / get on well and say which sounds more natural in American English (A2+ SLOs 3–4).
RecycleWk 1 hit it off · break the ice · make small talk · Wk 2 run into · head out · Wk 3 work it out · figure out · give up (on) · come up with · start over
OutcomeStudents talk about a relationship (real or invented) for 2–3 minutes using 2–3 expressions, with correct word order for stood me up / got stood up.

Schedule Change

Week 4 and Week 5 were swapped. Week 4 is now Relationships (originally Week 5) and Week 5 is the Review of Weeks 1–4, right before the Week 6 midterm. Tell students (and your program, if they have the syllabus dated 10/01/2026).

The old “Review (Weeks 1–4)” Parts 1–2 are now the corrected Warm-up A and B on the worksheet (Weeks 1–3).

Teaching Notes

get on well = British. Oxford Learner's Dictionaries labels get on with somebody British English; get along is the everyday American choice. It is still a Final core expression, so teach both, model get along, and accept either on the midterm and final.
stand someone up = informal. Oxford American and Britannica both label it informal. It often sounds like the person didn't care. For work: “Sorry, I missed the meeting.” Passive I got stood up is very common.
Personal topics are optional. Relationships can be sensitive. Every prompt has a “real or invented” option, the Relationship Map is optional, and all role-plays are neutral (roommates, neighbors, friends).

Procedure · Block 1

Stages A–E · 100 minutes
Block 1Teacher Copy
A · Warm-up & Spiral ReviewPairs → solo0:00–0:15
  1. Phrase Journal share (5 min, pairs). Partner asks: “Where did you hear it? Was it casual or neutral?” Collect 2–3 examples.
  2. Warm-up A–B (10 min). Students do the synonym match and 6 short stories alone (6 min), check with a partner, then you reveal answers on the slide. These replace the old “Review Weeks 1–4” Parts 1–2, with fixes: start over = “begin again” (not “restart”); no duplicate break the ice item; shoot the breeze is matched to “chat” (recognition only).
B · Engage: Who's in your circle?Pairs → class0:15–0:25

Pairs discuss Then / Now / Here (3 min): “Who was your best friend at 15? Do you still talk? Who do you see every day?” Allow a TV character or invented person. Then introduce Maple Street Apartments: five doors, five stories. Students predict what's behind each door. Don't teach the expressions yet.

C · Behind the Doors: New ExpressionsClass → pairs0:25–0:55
  1. Door stories (12 min). Tell each story, let students guess, then click to reveal. CCQs:
    DoorStoryCCQs (answers)
    1A RosaBest friends; Mei moved to Chicago; only birthday texts; no fight → grew apartDid they fight? (No) Fast or slowly? (Slowly)
    2B Ben & TariqVery different roommates; share cleaning; never argue → get alongDo they argue? (No) One day or a long time? (A long time)
    3C LucyDinner at 7; waited an hour; friend never came or texted → stood her up / got stood upDid her friend come? (No) Tell her? (No) Who got stood up? (Lucy)
    4D Mr. KimFrom London: “We get on really well” → get on well = get alongAmerican or British? (British) What does the neighbor say? (get along)
    5E Ana & SofiaSisters; argument; 2 months of silence; Ana said sorry → patched things upClose before? (Yes) Close now? (Yes) A problem in the middle? (Yes)
    Then pairs go through the Expression Cards (guess → Reveal).
  2. Grammar discovery (6 min). Slide: four sentences; pairs decide which are correct before you reveal. Elicit: the person goes in the middle (like figure it out); passive got stood up; don't forget with.
  3. American or British? (4 min). We get along well (US) vs. We get on well (UK). Both correct on tests.
  4. Pronunciation (4 min). grow aPART · get aLONG (ge-da-LONG) · got stood UP (gat-stoo-DUP) · patch things UP (patch-thing-ZUP).
  5. Register (4 min). get along: fine everywhere, even interviews. stand someone up: friends yes; at work say “I missed the meeting.”

Listening · Controlled practice
Block 1Teacher Copy
D · Listening: “The Reunion”Class → pairs0:55–1:10

Before (3 min): What is a reunion? Would you go? (optional) · Predict: how does Lucy feel? Listen 1 (3 min): True/False. Listen 2 (3 min): catch the expression; then two meaning questions. After (6 min): pairs advise Lucy; B1 retell in 3 sentences. Read the dialogue live with a confident student at natural speed; students can use the Listen button at home (device voice). The transcript is hidden on the student page until they tap it.

Lucy:Omar, look at this. I got an invitation to my ten-year high school reunion.
Omar:Oh, fun! Are you going?
Lucy:I don't know. My best friend from high school will be there. Jess. We were so close. We did everything together.
Omar:So what happened?
Lucy:Nothing, really. She moved to California for college, I stayed here… We just grew apart.
Omar:That happens. Do you still talk?
Lucy:Not really. Actually, last year we made plans to meet for coffee when she was in town, and she stood me up.
Omar:Ouch. She just didn't come?
Lucy:Nope. I waited for forty-five minutes. Later she texted, “So sorry, something came up.”
Omar:Hmm. Maybe something really did come up. You should talk to her at the reunion. Maybe you can patch things up.
Lucy:Maybe. I mean, we got along so well for years. It's sad to give up on that.
Omar:Exactly. And who knows? You might hit it off again, like old times.
Lucy:Ha! Okay, okay. I'll think about it. What about you? Do you still talk to your high school friends?
Omar:Just one. We get along great. We still play basketball every Sunday.
Lucy:That's nice. Okay, I have to head out. My meeting starts in five minutes.
Omar:Go! And send Jess a message!

~210 words · about 75 seconds · targets: grew apart, stood me up, patch things up, got along, get along · recycled: give up on, hit it off, head out, something came up (Week 3 B1 bonus).

E · Controlled PracticeSolo → pairs1:10–1:40
  • Ex 1 Which Expression? (4 min, pairs). Match 5 situations.
  • Ex 2 Fill in the Blank (6 min, solo). Item 6 has two answers (along / on): ask “Which is American?”
  • Ex 3 Circle the Correct Option (4 min, pairs).
  • Ex 4 Who Got Stood Up? (5 min, solo). Active ↔ passive. Accept got stood up or was stood up, with or without by….
  • Check together (3 min). Drill the most-missed items.
  • Ex 8 Talk to Your Partner (8 min). New partner; one follow-up question per answer; real or invented.
A2+: keep the toolkit open; Ex 8 questions 1 and 4 with the frames.   B1: cover the toolkit for Ex 2 and 4; Ex 8 all four, two expressions per answer.

H · Behind the Doors: Role-playsPairs (rotate) → class0:25–0:55

  2. Round 1 (9 min). Each pair gets a door card (printed or page → Open a random door). 1 minute to read, no writing. A and B 4 min, then switch roles.

Language Notes

  • grow apart vs. grow up (become an adult) and break up (end a romantic relationship). B1 alternatives: drift apart, lose touch.
  • get along: past participle gotten along (American). hit it off = right away; get along = over time.
  • stand someone up vs. stand up (rise) and stand up for (defend).
  • patch things up: also patch it up, patch up your differences; American alternative make up (“They made up”).
