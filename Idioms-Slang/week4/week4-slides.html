Behind the Doors · Whole class → pairs

What’s the story behind each door?

Door 1A · Rosa

Best friends in high school. Mei moved to Chicago. Now they only text on birthdays. No fight.

They grew apart.

Door 2B · Ben & Tariq

Very different roommates. They share the cleaning and never argue.

They get along really well.

Door 3C · Lucy

Dinner plan, Friday at 7:00. She waited an hour. Her friend never came or texted.

Her friend stood her up. Lucy got stood up.

Door 4D · Mr. Kim

From London: “My new coworker and I… really well.”

“We get on really well.” (British) = “We get along.”

Door 5E · Ana & Sofia

Sisters. Big argument. Two months of silence. Ana called and said sorry.

They patched things up.

Grammar discovery · Whole class

Which ones are correct?

She stood me up.✓
She stood up me.✗
I got stood up.✓
I don’t get along my boss.✗

The person goes in the middle: stood me up (just like figure it out).
Passive: I got stood up. · Don’t forget with: get along with my boss.

American English · Whole class

American or British?

United States

We get along well.

The everyday American choice: friends, work, interviews.

United Kingdom

We get on well.

Americans understand it, but it sounds British.

On the midterm and final, both are correct. In conversation here, get along sounds more natural.

Pronunciation · Whole class → pairs

Say it naturally

Stress the last part

get aLONG
stood me UP

Link the words

get along → ge-da-LONG
got stood up → gat-stoo-DUP
get on well → ge-don-WELL
patch things up → patch-thing-ZUP

Say it to your partner: “I got stood up!” · “We get along really well.”

Register · Worksheet Ex 7 → whole class

Would you say this? Stand up or stay seated

To a close friend: “Ugh, I got stood up last night!”Natural: stand up
In an email to your manager: “Sorry I stood you up at the meeting.”Not natural → “Sorry I missed the meeting.”
To your American neighbor: “I get along with everyone in the building.”Natural: stand up
To your teacher: “My classmate and I patched things up.”Natural: stand up
In a job interview: “I get along well with my coworkers.”Natural: stand up
To an American friend: “We get on well.”Not natural (British) → “We get along well.”
