Pair work

Stop and Talk

10–15 min
Walk, stop, talk to the closest person

How it works

  1. Everyone stands up and walks around the room. (Teacher plays music or claps.)
  2. When the teacher says “Stop!”, talk to the person closest to you.
  3. Read the question on the screen. Both partners answer. Use the expression in bold.
  4. Ask one follow-up question. You have 90 seconds.
  5. Teacher says “Walk!” and shows a new question. Find a new partner each time.
Useful language: Really? When was that? · What happened next? · How did you feel? · Me too! / Not me. I…
Teacher notes

Movement is the point here: keep students walking between rounds. 6–8 rounds work well. Personal answers are optional; students can always say “I'd rather not say” or invent an answer.

  • A2+: answer in 2–3 sentences; use the sentence starter on the screen.
  • B1: answer, then ask two follow-ups and use one more expression from another week.

Pair work

Role-Play Roulette

15 min
Spin a situation. Act it out. Can the class guess who you're talking to?

How it works

  1. Click Spin. You get a person, a situation, and 2 expressions.
  2. Stand up with your partner. Partner A starts. Talk for 2 minutes.
  3. Choose your words for that person: a boss is not a close friend!
  4. 2–3 pairs perform for the class without saying who the person is.
  5. The class guesses: Who were they talking to? How do you know?
Useful language: Hey! / Hi, sorry to bother you… · Could you…? / Can you…? · I'm so sorry, but… · Thanks so much! / I owe you one!
Teacher notes

This is a register activity in disguise: the class has to guess the relationship from how students speak (polite vs. casual). Spin a new combination for each pair, or one for the whole class. Switch roles after each performance.

  • A2+: use the useful language and the 2 expressions only.
  • B1: add a third expression and make the register clear without saying the person's name.

Group work

The Advice Wall

15–20 min
Walk the room. Read the problem. Leave your best advice.

How it works

  1. Six problems are on big papers around the room. Each group starts at one paper.
  2. Read the problem. Write one piece of advice with an expression from Weeks 1–4.
  3. You can't repeat an expression that's already on the paper!
  4. When the timer ends, move to the next paper (clockwise).
  5. At the end, go back to your first paper. Choose the best advice and read it to the class.
Useful language: You should… / You could… · Why don't you…? · If I were you, I'd… · Don't give up! Try…
Teacher notes

Before class (2 min): write the six problems on large paper or the whiteboard (they're on the screen too). Give each group a different color marker so you can see who wrote what. 3 minutes per paper; 4–6 rotations. Advice to a friend can be casual; check that the expressions are used correctly when groups read aloud.

  • A2+: use the starters and the expressions under each problem.
  • B1: give advice with two expressions and add a reason (“because…”).

Class discussion

Four Corners

15 min
Move to the corner that matches your opinion. Then defend it!

How it works

  1. Read the statement. Think for 10 seconds.
  2. Walk to a corner: Agree · Disagree · It depends · Not sure.
  3. In your corner, talk for 1 minute. Find your best reason. Use an expression.
  4. Each corner shares one reason with the class.
  5. Changed your mind? You can move to another corner. Tell us why!
Useful language: I agree, because… · I disagree. In my experience… · It depends on… · You changed my mind because…
Teacher notes

Label the four corners of the room (paper signs or point to them). Read one statement at a time. Encourage movement: anyone who changes corners explains why. Keep it light; there are no right answers.

  • A2+: one reason with the sentence starter.
  • B1: respond to another corner (“I see your point, but…”) and use two expressions.
