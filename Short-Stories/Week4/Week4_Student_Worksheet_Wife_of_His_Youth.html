Part 6

Literary Lens: Role on the Wall

Groups of 3–4 · at the wallCharacterization

Study: How do writers build a character?

Direct characterization: the narrator tells us (“Mr. Ryder was one of the most conservative”).

Indirect characterization: the writer shows us through Speech · Thoughts · Effect on others · Actions · Looks (STEAL).

An antihero is a main character without traditional heroic qualities, who still holds our interest or sympathy.

Your group draws the outline of a person on a large sheet of paper on the wall. Outside the outline: how Groveland and the Blue Veins see Mr. Ryder. Inside: what he thinks, feels, hides, or fears. Every note needs a § number. Then walk around and read the other groups’ posters. Copy your group’s best ideas here.

OUTSIDE: the public Mr. Ryder (§)INSIDE: the private Mr. Ryder (§)

STEAL check: Which method does Chesnutt use most for Mr. Ryder? Give one example.

Close Reading: Part III (§8–§9)

Individualthen Pairs

Now read §8–§9 silently. Then answer with section evidence.

  1. Who were the guests at the ball, and what did they have in common? (§8)
  2. Mr. Ryder tells ’Liza Jane’s story “in the same soft Southern dialect, which came easily to his lips.” Why is this detail important? (§8)

  6. Look at the last sentence. Why does Mr. Ryder call her “the wife of my youth” instead of using her name? (§9)
Part 11

Author’s Style: Juxtaposition Sort and Carousel

Groups · card sortthen Carousel · moving

Study: Juxtaposition

Juxtaposition = placing two very different things side by side so that the contrast creates meaning. Ask three questions: (1) What two things are placed together? (2) How are they different (or surprisingly similar)? (3) What does the contrast reveal about racial identity, class, or character?

Step 1 · Card sort (5 min): Your group gets 14 cards. Match them into 7 pairs that Chesnutt places side by side. Step 2 · Carousel (9 min): Each group starts at a poster with one pair. Write what the contrast reveals. Every 3 minutes, move to the next poster, read what is there, and add a new idea or a piece of evidence.
