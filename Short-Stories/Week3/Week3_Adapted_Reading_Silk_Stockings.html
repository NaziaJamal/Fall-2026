Before You Read

About the author

Kate Chopin (1850–1904) was born in St. Louis, Missouri. She married a Louisiana cotton businessman and lived in New Orleans. When her husband died, she was in her early thirties with six children to support. She moved back to St. Louis and began to write. She published nearly 100 stories, many about the Creole and Cajun people of Louisiana.

Her novel The Awakening (1899), about a married woman who wants freedom, shocked many critics. Today Chopin is admired for her honest, modern view of women’s inner lives. “A Pair of Silk Stockings” was first published in Vogue magazine in September 1897.

The world of the story

The story takes place in a large American city in the 1890s. New department stores sold everything in one building, and cable cars carried shoppers downtown. In most families, a married woman’s main job was to care for her husband and children, and money for herself came last.

How much is $15? In the 1890s, one dollar was worth roughly forty of today’s dollars, so Mrs. Sommers’s $15 is roughly $600 today. Her silk stockings cost $1.98 (about $80 today).

Words from the 1890s
stockingsLong, thin coverings for the feet and legs, worn by women under long skirts. Cotton stockings were cheap; silk stockings were a luxury.
a bargain counterA table in a store where goods are sold at reduced prices. Crowds of shoppers pushed to reach it.
kid glovesGloves made of very soft leather from young goats. Expensive gloves were “fitted” to a customer’s hand by a clerk.
a matineeAn afternoon performance at a theater.
a cable carA city streetcar pulled along a track by a moving cable under the street.

As you read, notice: what Mrs. Sommers plans and what she does · what she touches, tastes, sees, and hears · moments when we hear her thoughts directly · the moment when her day changes direction.

Reading 1 · §1–§3
§1Fifteen dollars

Little Mrs. Sommers one day found herself the unexpected owner of fifteen dollars. It seemed to her a very large amount of money, and the way it stuffed and bulged her worn old purse gave her a feeling of importance such as she had not enjoyed for years.

The question of how to spend it occupied her greatly. For a day or two she walked about apparently in a dreamy state, but really absorbed in planning and calculation. She did not wish to act hastily, to do anything she might afterward regret. But it was during the still hours of the night, when she lay awake turning plans over in her mind, that she seemed to see her way clearly toward a proper and judicious use of the money.

A dollar or two should be added to the price usually paid for Janie’s shoes, which would make them last a good deal longer than they usually did. She would buy so many yards of cotton cloth for new shirts for the boys and Janie and Mag. She had intended to make the old ones do by skillful patching. Mag should have another dress. She had seen some beautiful patterns, real bargains, in the shop windows. And still there would be enough left for new stockings — two pairs each — and what mending that would save for a while! She would get caps for the boys and sailor hats for the girls. The vision of her little brood looking fresh and dainty and new for once in their lives excited her and made her restless and wakeful with anticipation.

The neighbors sometimes talked of certain “better days” that little Mrs. Sommers had known before she had ever thought of being Mrs. Sommers. She herself did not indulge in such gloomy looking back. She had no time — no second of time — to give to the past. The needs of the present absorbed every part of her mind. A vision of the future like some dim, gaunt monster sometimes frightened her, but luckily tomorrow never comes.

Word notesabsorbed giving all your attention to something · judicious wise and sensible · make the old ones do continue using old things because there is no other choice · patching / mending repairing holes in clothes · brood a family of young children (literally, young birds) · dainty pretty and delicate · indulge in allow yourself to enjoy something · gaunt very thin, like someone who is starving
§2At the counter

Mrs. Sommers was one who knew the value of bargains. She could stand for hours making her way inch by inch toward the desired object that was selling below cost. She could push with her elbows if she needed to; she had learned to grab a piece of goods and hold it and stick to it with persistence and determination until her turn came to be served, no matter when it came.

But that day she was a little faint and tired. She had swallowed a light lunch — no! when she came to think of it, between getting the children fed and the place cleaned up, and preparing herself for the shopping battle, she had actually forgotten to eat any lunch at all!

She sat down on a revolving stool in front of a counter that was almost empty, trying to gather strength and courage to charge through an eager crowd that was attacking walls of shirt cloth and printed cotton like soldiers attacking a fort. An all-gone, limp feeling had come over her, and she rested her hand aimlessly upon the counter. She wore no gloves. Little by little she became aware that her hand had touched something very soothing, very pleasant to touch. She looked down to see that her hand lay upon a pile of silk stockings.

Word notespersistence continuing to try even when it is difficult · faint weak and dizzy, as if you might fall · limp soft and weak, without energy · aimlessly without any purpose · soothing making you feel calm and comfortable
§3The silk stockings

A sign nearby announced that they had been reduced in price from two dollars and fifty cents to one dollar and ninety-eight cents, and a young girl who stood behind the counter asked her if she wished to examine their line of silk stockings. She smiled, just as if she had been asked to inspect a crown of diamonds with the final purpose of buying it. But she went on feeling the soft, shiny, luxurious things — with both hands now — holding them up to see them shine and to feel them glide serpent-like through her fingers.

Two bright red spots came suddenly into her pale cheeks. She looked up at the girl.

“Do you think there are any eights-and-a-half among these?”

There were any number of eights-and-a-half. In fact, there were more of that size than any other. Here was a light-blue pair; there were some lavender, some all black, and various shades of tan and gray. Mrs. Sommers chose a black pair and looked at them very long and closely. She pretended to be examining their quality, which the clerk assured her was excellent.

“A dollar and ninety-eight cents,” she said aloud, thoughtfully. “Well, I’ll take this pair.” She handed the girl a five-dollar bill and waited for her change and for her package. What a very small package it was! It seemed lost in the depths of her shabby old shopping bag.

Word notesluxurious very comfortable and expensive · glide move smoothly and quietly · serpent-like like a snake (notice: in Week 2, the serpent was also a sign of temptation) · eights-and-a-half her stocking size · clerk a person who sells things in a store · shabby old and in bad condition from long use

Reading 2 · §4–§8
§4The impulse

After that, Mrs. Sommers did not move in the direction of the bargain counter. She took the elevator, which carried her to an upper floor, into the area of the ladies’ waiting rooms. Here, in a quiet corner, she exchanged her cotton stockings for the new silk ones which she had just bought. She was not going through any sharp mental process or reasoning with herself, nor was she trying to explain to her own satisfaction the motive of her action. She was not thinking at all. She seemed for the time to be taking a rest from that hard and tiring job, and to have given herself up to some mechanical impulse that directed her actions and freed her of responsibility.

How good was the touch of the raw silk to her flesh! She felt like lying back in the cushioned chair and enjoying the luxury of it for a while. She did, for a little while. Then she put her shoes back on, rolled the cotton stockings together, and pushed them into her bag. After doing this, she crossed straight over to the shoe department and took her seat to be fitted.

She was fastidious. The clerk could not understand her; he could not make her shoes match her stockings, and she was not easily pleased. She held back her skirts and turned her feet one way and her head another way as she looked down at the polished, pointed-toe boots. Her foot and ankle looked very pretty. She could not realize that they belonged to her and were a part of herself. She wanted an excellent and stylish fit, she told the young man who served her, and she did not mind the difference of a dollar or two more in the price so long as she got what she wanted.

Word notesmotive the reason for doing something · impulse a sudden strong wish to do something, without thinking · mechanical automatic, like a machine · raw silk natural, untreated silk · fastidious very careful about small details and hard to please · fitted measured so that something is the right size
§5Gloves and magazines

It was a long time since Mrs. Sommers had been fitted with gloves. On the rare occasions when she had bought a pair, they were always “bargains,” so cheap that it would have been ridiculous and unreasonable to expect them to be fitted to the hand.

Now she rested her elbow on the cushion of the glove counter, and a pretty, pleasant young woman, delicate and skillful with her hands, drew a long-wristed kid glove over Mrs. Sommers’s hand. She smoothed it down over the wrist and buttoned it neatly, and both lost themselves for a second or two in admiring the small, perfectly shaped gloved hand. But there were other places where money might be spent.

There were books and magazines piled up in the window of a stand a few steps down the street. Mrs. Sommers bought two high-priced magazines such as she had been used to reading in the days when she had been used to other pleasant things. She carried them without wrapping. As well as she could, she lifted her skirts at the street crossings. Her stockings and boots and well-fitting gloves had worked wonders in the way she carried herself — had given her a feeling of assurance, a sense of belonging to the well-dressed crowd.

Word notesridiculous silly; not reasonable · admiring looking at something with pleasure and respect · high-priced expensive · worked wonders had an excellent effect · assurance confidence; a feeling of being sure of yourself
§6The restaurant

She was very hungry. Another time she would have ignored her hunger until she got home, where she would have made herself a cup of tea and eaten a snack of anything that was available. But the impulse that was guiding her would not allow her to consider any such thought.

There was a restaurant at the corner. She had never entered its doors; from the outside she had sometimes caught glimpses of spotless white tablecloths and shining crystal, and soft-stepping waiters serving fashionable people.

When she entered, her appearance created no surprise, no alarm, as she had half feared it might. She sat down at a small table alone, and an attentive waiter at once came to take her order. She did not want a large amount; she craved a nice and tasty bite — half a dozen oysters, a plump lamb chop with salad greens, something sweet — a frozen dessert, for instance; a glass of German white wine, and at the end a small cup of black coffee.

While waiting to be served, she removed her gloves very slowly and laid them beside her. Then she picked up a magazine and looked through it, cutting the pages open with the blunt edge of her knife. It was all very agreeable. The tablecloth was even more spotless than it had seemed through the window, and the crystal more sparkling. There were quiet ladies and gentlemen, who did not notice her, eating lunch at small tables like her own. A soft, pleasing piece of music could be heard, and a gentle breeze was blowing through the window. She tasted a bite, and she read a word or two, and she sipped the amber wine and wiggled her toes in the silk stockings. The price of it made no difference. She counted the money out to the waiter and left an extra coin on his tray, and he bowed before her as before a princess of royal blood.

Word notesglimpses quick, short looks · crystal expensive glass · alarm sudden worry or fear · crave to want something very strongly · cutting the pages open in the 1890s, some magazine pages were joined at the edges and had to be cut before reading · agreeable pleasant · amber golden-brown in color
§7The theater

There was still money in her purse, and her next temptation appeared in the shape of a poster for an afternoon play.

It was a little later when she entered the theater. The play had begun, and the house seemed to her to be full. But there were empty seats here and there, and she was shown to one of them, between brilliantly dressed women who had gone there to pass the time, eat candy, and show off their gaudy clothes. There were many others who were there only for the play and the acting. It is safe to say that no one there had quite the same attitude toward her surroundings as Mrs. Sommers did. She gathered in the whole thing — stage and actors and people — in one wide impression, and absorbed it and enjoyed it. She laughed at the comedy and wept — she and the gaudy woman next to her wept over the tragedy. And they talked a little together about it. And the gaudy woman wiped her eyes and sniffed into a tiny square of thin, perfumed lace and passed little Mrs. Sommers her box of candy.

Word notestemptation something that makes you want to do something you probably should not do · gaudy too bright and showy · impression the effect something has on your mind and feelings · wept past of weep, to cry
§8The cable car

The play was over, the music stopped, the crowd filed out. It was like a dream ended. People scattered in all directions. Mrs. Sommers went to the corner and waited for the cable car.

A man with sharp eyes, who sat opposite her, seemed to like studying her small, pale face. It puzzled him to understand what he saw there. In truth, he saw nothing — unless he were wizard enough to detect a deep, painful wish, a powerful longing that the cable car would never stop anywhere, but go on and on with her forever.

Word notesfiled out left in a line, one after another · scattered moved away in different directions · puzzled confused · detect to notice something that is hard to see · longing a strong wish for something you may not be able to have
