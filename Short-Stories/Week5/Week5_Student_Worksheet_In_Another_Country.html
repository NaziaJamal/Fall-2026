Literary Lens: Mood in the First Paragraph (§1)

Teacher reads aloudthen Pairs

Study: Mood

Mood is the feeling a text creates in the reader. Writers create mood with setting (time, weather, place), imagery (pictures for the senses), and word choice. (Tone is the writer’s attitude.)

Listen to §1. Then find the details and explain the mood they create.

Detail from §1Sense / typeMood it creates

Part 6

Author’s Style: The Iceberg Wall

Pairs · at the wallInference

Study: The iceberg principle

Hemingway said that a writer can leave things out, and if he writes truly enough, the reader will still feel them. Only one-eighth of an iceberg is above the water. Above the surface = what the words say. Below the surface = what the characters feel, hide, or cannot say.

Your teacher gives your pair two quotation cards. On one color of sticky note, write what is above the surface (the facts). On another color, write what is below (the hidden feeling or meaning). Stick your notes in the right place on the big iceberg on the wall. Then read the other pairs’ notes and copy the three best here.

Part 9

Author’s Style: The Hemingway Edit

PairsStyle · Production

Study: Minimalism and understatement

Minimalism: short, simple, concrete sentences; few adjectives; repetition instead of variety; feelings shown through actions, not named.

Understatement: describing something as smaller or calmer than it really is (“It is very difficult”), which makes the feeling stronger.

Hemingway once said that a writer should begin with “one true sentence.”

Below is an over-written version of one moment from §8. It is NOT Hemingway. With your partner, edit it: cut adjectives, remove explanations of feelings, use simple, concrete actions. Aim for 60 words or fewer. Then compare your version with Hemingway’s in §8.

Overcome by an unbearable, crushing wave of grief that seemed to tear his poor, broken heart into a thousand tiny, bleeding pieces, the brave and noble major, who had suffered so terribly and so unfairly, suddenly began to weep with great, heartbreaking sobs. Hot, bitter tears of agony streamed endlessly down his pale, tragic face like a river of sorrow as he desperately tried, with all the remaining strength of his wounded soul, to remain dignified and proud. Despite his overwhelming, unimaginable pain, he forced his trembling body to stand as straight as a soldier on parade, and, with his head held high and his eyes staring blindly into the empty, meaningless distance, he walked slowly and heroically past the cold, cruel machines and disappeared through the door, leaving behind him a room filled with silent, shocked sadness.

Over-written version: 138 words. Hemingway’s version of the same moment is about 60 words.

Our edit

Three things we cut, and why
Hemingway’s version (§8): what does he keep that we cut? What does he cut that we kept?
Which version makes the reader feel more? Why?
