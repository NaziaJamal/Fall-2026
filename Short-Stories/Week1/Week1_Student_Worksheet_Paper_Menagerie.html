Literary Lens: Motif

PairsSemi-controlled

Study: What is a motif?

A motif is an image, object, idea, or phrase that appears again and again in a story. Each time it returns, its meaning can grow or change. Readers track a motif to discover the story’s theme.

Three questions for any motif: (1) Where does it appear? (2) What happens to it? (3) What does it mean at this moment?

Part 7

Author’s Style: Figurative Language

PairsSemi-controlled

Study: Three tools of figurative language

Simile — compares two different things using like or as.

Metaphor — compares two things directly, without like or as; one thing is or acts as another.

Personification — gives human feelings or actions to an animal, object, or idea.

Analysis formula: The writer uses [tool] when [quote/§]. This suggests [meaning] and makes the reader feel [effect].

Find each passage. Copy the key words (no more than one line), name the tool, and explain its effect.

Think deeper

Laohu laughs, purrs, and seems to feel guilt. Is this personification — or is the magic real in this story? Does it matter? Write one sentence for your opinion.

Part 10

Seminar Discussion

Whole class

Seminar grades are based on the quality of your contributions: depth, evidence, and building on others’ ideas — not how often you speak.

  1. Why does Liu call the story “The Paper Menagerie” instead of “The Paper Animals” or “My Mother’s Toys”?
  2. Is this an American story? Who is “the American self” here — Jack, Mom, or both?
  3. Mom’s life story is told only at the very end. Why does Liu wait so long to let her speak? What is the effect?
  4. Is the ending hopeful or tragic? Can it be both?
  5. Does this story describe only one family, or a wider experience? Connect it to your own observations.
