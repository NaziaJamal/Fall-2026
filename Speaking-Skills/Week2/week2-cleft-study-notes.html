Cleft Sentences · Study Notes

Week 2 · Your Idea · How to put the spotlight on your main message

1What is a cleft sentence?

A cleft sentence takes one simple sentence and splits it into two parts ("cleft" means split). This puts a spotlight on the most important information.

Simple sentenceThe first follower matters.
Cleft sentenceWhat really matters is the first follower.

Same information, but the cleft sentence tells the audience: "Listen, this is the important part."

2Why do speakers use them?

  • Focus: the key information comes at the end, after a short wait. The audience pays more attention.
  • Clarity: it shows exactly what your main point is. That's perfect for stating your idea.
  • Contrast: it's easy to correct a wrong idea: It isn't talent that matters; it's practice.
  • Confidence: it sounds organized and sure, like a TED speaker.
In your talk: use one or two clefts for your big moments (your main idea, a key contrast, your final message). If every sentence is a cleft, nothing stands out.

3The four patterns

① What-cleftWhat + subject + verb + be + FOCUS

Use it to introduce your main point. The focus can be a noun, a that-clause, or an action.

What I want you to remember is this.
What surprised me most was how fast the crowd grew.
What I've learned is that asking for help is a skill.
What he did was (to) start dancing alone. ← with do, the focus is an action
② It-cleftIt + be + FOCUS + that / who + rest of sentence

Use it to point to one person, thing, time or place. Use who for people (or that), that for things.

It's the first follower who changes everything.
It was in my first week at college that I learned this.
Derek Sivers, 2:27
"It was really the first follower that transformed the lone nut into a leader."
③ The thing / reason / person / place…The + noun + (that) clause + be + FOCUS

A flexible version of the what-cleft. Choose the noun that fits: thing, reason, problem, question, person, place, moment, way.

The reason this matters is that anyone can do it.
The problem is that we don't ask.
The person who changed my mind was my grandmother.
The only thing that truly matters is having something worth saying.
④ All-cleftAll + subject + verb + be + FOCUS

All here means "the only thing". Use it to make a request sound small and easy.

All I'm asking is one minute a day.
All you need is one person to follow you.
B2+ · Reversed what-cleft: put the focus first for a strong ending: The first follower is what transforms a lone nut into a leader. (Sivers, 0:55). Also: That's what I want you to remember.

4How to make one: four steps

Start with a simple sentence: Practice makes a great speaker.

  1. Choose the focus: the part you want to stand out → practice
  2. Choose a pattern → what-cleft? it-cleft?
  3. Build it → It's practice that makes a great speaker. / What makes a great speaker is practice.
  4. Check the verb be: does the tense match the story? (see section 5)

5Grammar checklist

  • Tense of be: usually match the tense of the story. Present: What matters is… · Past: What mattered was… · It was my teacher who…
  • Singular be after what, even with a plural focus: What we need is more examples. (Many speakers also say are here; is is always safe.)
  • No extra pronoun in the what part: What I want it is…
  • Don't drop that / who in an it-cleft when it is the subject: It's my mother who taught me.
  • A that-clause after is when the focus is a full idea: The reason is that… (not The reason is because… in formal speech)

Common mistakes

✗ Don't say✓ Say
What I want it is a clear idea.What I want is a clear idea.
It's my mother taught me to listen.It's my mother who taught me to listen.
Yesterday, what surprised me is the crowd.Yesterday, what surprised me was the crowd.
The thing what I love is stories.The thing (that) I love is stories.
What is important it is practice.What is important is practice.

6Say it like a speaker

The grammar does half the work. Your voice does the other half:

  • Pause just before the focus.
  • Stress the most important word in the focus.
  • Let your voice fall at the end. It sounds sure.
What really matters | is the FIRST follower.
It isn't TALENT that matters | it's PRACTICE.

| = short pause · CAPITALS = stress

8Check yourself

Rewrite each sentence with the pattern in brackets. Press Show answers at the top when you finish.

1
Stories help people remember. (What…)
✓ What helps people remember is stories.
2
My teacher gave me the courage to speak. (It was…)
✓ It was my teacher who gave me the courage to speak.
3
We need one clear idea. (All…)
✓ All we need is one clear idea.
4
People don't follow because joining feels risky. (The reason…)
✓ The reason people don't follow is that joining feels risky.
5
The crowd, not the leader, created the movement. (It wasn't… it was…)
✓ It wasn't the leader that created the movement; it was the crowd.
6
Find and fix the mistake: What I learned it is that small steps matter.
✓ What I learned is that small steps matter. (Remove "it"; for a past story, "What I learned was…" also works.)
