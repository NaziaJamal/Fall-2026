Overview

The big idea for today

An idea only spreads when other people can understand it, and follow it.

Anderson says the speaker's job is to rebuild an idea inside the listener's mind. Sivers shows that a movement only starts when the first person follows. Today, every student finds their idea and says it so clearly that a partner can "follow" it.

By the end of class, students can…

  • Explain what Anderson means by an idea and why he calls it a gift
  • Identify the core idea of a short talk in one sentence
  • Use cleft sentences to put focus on their key message
  • State their own idea in one clear sentence and test it with a partner
  • Complete an Idea Blueprint for their final talk

Sophie's Laugh + One Breath

Engage6:00–6:15
GoalHook students with a real "gift" talk, then show them why an idea must be short and clear
InteractionWhole class → pairs → whole class
MaterialsSophie Scott clip (90 sec) · slides 3–4 · worksheet A1–A2
Teacher instructions
  1. 2 minPlay the clip. Don't introduce the topic. Just say: "This is a scientist at TED in 2015. Watch."
  2. 4 minPairs discuss A1: Did you laugh? Why? What will her big idea about laughter be? (B2+: what did she do to make you keep listening?)
    Don't tell anyone her idea. You'll find out in the reading.
  3. 2 minQuick share: collect 3 predictions and write them on the board. Leave them there for the reading.
  4. 7 minOne Breath (A2). Bridge: "Sophie made a thousand people laugh and learn. Can you make one person understand your idea in one breath?" Model one sample idea in one breath, then pairs: A says their Week 1 idea in one breath, B repeats it in their own words, then swap. Debrief: What made an idea easy to repeat? Write a short list on the board (one point · simple words · a reason to care).
Student instructions
Watch and discuss A1. Then say your idea in one breath. Your partner repeats it in their own words.
Expected output: 3 predictions on the board; every student says their idea once and hears it repeated.

B2 support

Frames on the slide: "My idea is that…" / "So you're saying that…". No Week 1 idea yet? Use: "Something I wish everyone knew is…"

B2+ challenge

One breath with no "and" or "but". The listener guesses who the idea is for.

Three Rooms Jigsaw

Study6:30–7:05
GoalUnderstand Anderson's idea of an idea, and apply it to their own life
InteractionPairs → expert groups → mixed groups → individual → pairs
MaterialsPrinted Parts A, B, C · worksheet B1–B5 · slides 7–12
Teacher instructions
  1. 3 minPredict (B1). Show the subtitle "The Gift in Every Great Talk." Pairs: What gift can a talk give? Remember Anderson's talk last week.
  2. 10 minExpert groups (B2). Groups A, B, C read their part and complete the expert card. Walk around; check each expert can explain the message without reading. Point B2 students to the glossary.
  3. 10 minTeaching groups (B3). One A + one B + one C per group. Each expert teaches for 3 minutes; others take notes. The group writes one summary sentence.
  4. 4 minTrue/false (B4). Individually, then Check answers. Go back to the board: were the Sophie predictions right? (Her idea: laughter turns social stress into pleasant connection.)
  5. 8 minFind your idea (B5). Each student picks two of Anderson's discovery questions and answers them to a partner. The partner listens and suggests: "I think your idea might be…"
What each part is about (for the teacher)
PartMain pointMake sure students notice
ASophie's talk changed how Anderson sees laughter forever. That is the "gift": an idea the listener keeps. His own difficult TED talk worked because it planted one idea: TED is special because people from every field come together and understand each other.The speaker's mission: take something that matters to you and rebuild it in the listener's mind.
BAnyone with an idea worth sharing can give a powerful talk; what matters is having something worth saying, not confidence or smooth talking. An idea can be simple: a how-to, a story with an insight, an image, a hope, a reminder. Some people overrate their ideas; many underrate them."An idea is anything that can change how people see the world." The warning about style without substance.
CFeeling you have nothing to say may just be low confidence. Other people may see value you can't see. Your experience is unique. A personal story with a simple lesson can make a great talk. Questions help you find your idea.The paradox (you only see yourself from the inside) and the discovery questions.
Expected output: group summary sentences; B4 completed; each student has 1–2 possible ideas.

B2 support

Parts B and C, glossary on the worksheet, expert card with starters. Experts may bring key-word notes to the teaching group.

B2+ challenge

Part A. Explain why Anderson tells his own TED story here. Is "style without substance is awful" too harsh?
Sensitive moment: Part B says people who overrate their ideas are "often male" and those who underrate them "often female". If it comes up, turn it to "Do you underestimate your ideas?" rather than a gender debate.

Watch & Analyze: Derek Sivers

Study7:05–7:40
GoalFind a talk's core idea and see how a short talk builds it step by step
InteractionPairs → individual → groups → whole class
MaterialsSivers talk (~3 min) · worksheet C1–C7 · slides 13–18
Teacher instructions
  1. 2 minPredict (C1) from the title only.
  2. 6 minViewing 1, no sound (C2). Students order the 7 events, then retell the story to a partner: "First…, then…". Ask: "Without words, what do you think his idea is?"
  3. 6 minViewing 2, with sound (C3). Choose the core idea (answer: c), then write it in their own words in 15 words or fewer. Pairs compare.
  4. 6 minViewing 3 (C4). Gap-fill: 10 one-word gaps, captions off. Pause briefly after 1:28 and 2:20 if needed. Then Check answers.
  5. 10 minGroups (C5 + C6). How he builds the idea (start, video, steps, call to action), then Anderson's four tests with evidence. Assign C5 to half the groups and C6 to the other half to save time, then they swap answers.
  6. 5 minClass discussion (C7). "Leadership is over-glorified." Students stand on a line: agree on one side of the room, disagree on the other. Ask 2–3 people from each end for a reason or example.

Cleft Sentences: Put the Spotlight on Your Idea

Study → Activate8:00–8:35
GoalUse cleft sentences to give focus and emphasis to a key message
InteractionPairs → teacher-led → individual → pairs
MaterialsSlides 20–23 · worksheet D1–D5
Teacher instructions
  1. 6 minNotice (D1). Board: a) The first follower matters. b) What really matters is the first follower. Which sounds like a TED speaker? Then students find the focus in three real sentences: two from Sivers (0:55 and 2:27) and one from Anderson's Part A.
  2. 7 minExplain (D2). Four patterns: what-cleft, it-cleft, "the reason/thing… is", all-cleft.
    The cleft makes the audience wait. The important part comes at the end, after a small pause.
    Model: "What really matters | is the FIRST follower." Students repeat chorally.
  3. 7 minControlled (D3). Rewrite 6 sentences, compare in pairs, then Show model answers.
  4. 6 minSemi-controlled (D4). Complete 4–5 cleft frames about the video and their opinions. Say two aloud to a partner with the pause.
  5. 9 minFreer (D5). Each student writes their own idea as three clefts. Partners read them aloud; the listener chooses the strongest and explains: "Your second one is stronger because…"
Expected output: D3 correct; 4–5 personal clefts; one strongest cleft version of their own idea.

B2 support

Focus on what-clefts and "the reason… is". Frame: "What I've learned is that ___."

B2+ challenge

All four patterns, plus contrast: "It isn't ___ that matters; it's ___."
Common errors: double subject ("What I want it is…"); missing that/who in it-clefts; tense ("What mattered is…" → "What mattered was…").

First Follower Pitch Rounds

Activate8:35–9:15
GoalPitch their idea, get feedback, and make it clearer each round
InteractionIndividual → rotating pairs (two lines) → whole class
MaterialsProjected timer · slides 24–25 · worksheet E1–E3
Teacher instructions
  1. 5 minPlan (E1). Key words only: idea (best cleft from D5), one example or story, why the listener should care.
  2. 2 minSet up two lines.
    You are the leader. Your partner decides whether to be your first follower. Make your idea easy to follow.
  3. 8 minRound 1. 60-second pitch → listener repeats the idea and says "I'd follow because…" or "I'm not following yet because…" → swap → Line B moves. Students log feedback in E2.
  4. 2 minRevise one thing.
  5. 8 minRound 2. New partner. The listener also asks one question.
  6. 2 minRevise again.
  7. 7 minRound 3. New partner. 45 seconds: shorter and sharper.
  8. 6 minClass discussion (E3). "Whose idea (not yours) would you follow? Why?" 2–3 volunteers pitch to the whole class; the class says what made it easy to follow.
Expected output: each student pitches 3 times and revises twice; E2 log completed.

B2 support

Use the feedback frames in E2. Students may hold a card with their cleft sentence in rounds 1–2.

B2+ challenge

No frames: diplomatic feedback in their own words. In round 3, the listener asks: "What would someone who disagrees say?"
Odd number? Join a line yourself, or make one student the observer who writes down the best sentence they hear.

D1 · Notice (the focus)

  1. It-cleft · focus: the first follower
  2. Reversed what-cleft · focus: the first follower (= What transforms a lone nut into a leader is the first follower.)
  3. What-cleft · focus: that a special way of sharing knowledge should be preserved

Why use them? To give focus and emphasis, create a short wait before the key point, and contrast it with something else.

D3 · Controlled practice

#PromptModel answer
1The first follower changes everything. (It…)It's the first follower who/that changes everything.
2I want you to remember one thing: practice. (What…)What I want you to remember is practice.
3A clear idea matters, not confidence. (It isn't… it's…)It isn't confidence that matters; it's a clear idea.
4This matters because everyone can do it. (The reason…)The reason this matters is that everyone can do it.
5I'm only asking for five minutes a day. (All…)All I'm asking for is five minutes a day.
6My grandmother taught me to listen. (It was…)It was my grandmother who taught me to listen.
