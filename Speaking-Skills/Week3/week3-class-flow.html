Step 1 · Warm-up · Worksheet A1

Gift or Ask?

Engage · 15 min

How it works

  • Listen to 8 talk openings
  • Vote: one hand = Gift · two hands = Ask
  • Partner talk: What's wrong? How would you fix it?
A gift"Why do we remember some days forever, but forget whole months? The answer surprised me."

Think about it

  • A gift gives the audience something: an idea, a tool, a new way to see
  • An ask wants something from them: money, attention, applause
An ask"Everything I'm going to talk about is in my new online course. It's 50% off this week only…"

Step 3 · Guided Reading · The big ideas

Four traps, four fixes

Study
1

The Sales Pitch

Using the talk to sell yourself or your business.

Give, don't take.

2

The Ramble

No preparation, no clear direction.

Prepare. Respect their time.

3

The Org Bore

All about your organization: history, team, products.

Talk about the work and the ideas.

4

The Inspiration Performance

Acting inspiring, chasing applause, little substance.

Earn it with real work and humility.

Anderson's rule: "Give, don't take." A talk is a gift, not an ask.

Step 5 · Language Focus · Worksheet D3–D5

Say them like a speaker

Study → Activate

Tips

  • Preview, then follow it: say "three", give three
  • Pause after the signpost
  • Short and natural: "So, …" "Now, …"
  • Use them at the turns, not every sentence

Watch out

  • ✗ Firstly… Secondly… → ✓ First… The second thing is…
  • ✗ "Finally" in the middle of the talk
  • ✗ "In conclusion, I would like to conclude…" → ✓ So, to sum up…

Practice

Second,|it helps you make friends.

Step 7 · Build Your Talk · Worksheet F

Test your throughline

Activate · 30 min
  1. Do I really care about this topic?
  2. Will it make people curious?
  3. Will it make a difference to them?
  4. Is it a gift or an ask?
  5. Is it fresh?
  6. Can I explain it in my time?
  7. Do I know enough?
  8. Do I have the credibility?
  9. What are my 15 words?
  10. Would they make someone want to listen?
