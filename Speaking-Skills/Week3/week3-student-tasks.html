Warm-up: Gift or Ask?

15 minPairs → ClassWorksheet A1

Goal: notice what makes a talk feel like a gift, and what makes it feel like an ask.

  1. Listen to each talk opening your teacher shows.
  2. With your partner, decide: is the speaker giving you something, or asking for something (your money, attention or applause)?
  3. Say what is wrong and how you would fix it.
  4. Vote with your hand: one hand up = Gift, two hands up = Ask.

It feels like a gift because it promises…

Reading: the Four Traps jigsaw

40 minGroupsWorksheet B1–B5

Goal: learn four ways talks go wrong, so you can avoid them in yours.

1 · The Sales Pitch

Selling yourself or your business.

2 · The Ramble

No preparation, no direction.

3 · The Org Bore

All about your organization.

4 · The Inspiration Performance

Acting inspiring, with little substance.

Steps

  1. Predict: what makes a talk bad? 3 min
  2. Expert group: read your trap card and complete the expert card. 10 min
  3. Teaching group: join one expert from each trap. Teach your trap in 2–3 minutes, without reading. 12 min
  4. Spot the trap: worksheet B4. 5 min
  5. Bad Talk Charades: draw a card with a secret trap and a topic. Plan a 30-second terrible talk, perform it, the class guesses, then give a fixed opening line. 10 min

Language: signposting

35 minIndividual → PairsWorksheet D1–D5

Goal: use short phrases that show your audience where you are in your talk.

JobExample
Introduce your ideaToday I want to talk about… · The idea is simple: …
PreviewI'll share three things I learned.
Order and add pointsFirst, … · I also noticed… · Here's one last thing…
ExampleFor example, …
Move onThat brings me to… · Now let's look at…
EndSo, to sum up, … · So here's my question to you: …

Steps

  1. Notice the signposts in the Cutts talk (D1).
  2. Choose the best signpost in a mini-talk (D3).
  3. Fix the ramble: add at least four signposts (D4).
  4. Write four signposts for your own talk (D5).
B2+ challenge: use a cleft to emphasize your key point: "What really matters is…"

Speaking: Talk Map trios

35 minGroups of 3Worksheet E1–E3

Goal: give a 90-second mini-talk so clear that your listener can draw its map.

Speaker

Give your 90-second talk: throughline, 2–3 points, ending. Use signposts.

Mapper

Draw the talk map: throughline, points in order, signposts heard.

Listener

Ask one question. Check for traps.
  1. Plan your mini-talk (E1). 5 min
  2. Round 1: each person speaks once; roles rotate. 15 min
  3. Compare the map with your plan. Fix one thing (E3). 3 min
  4. Round 2 in a new trio, a little shorter and clearer. 12 min

Map check

Your throughline was clear when you said…

I could follow you because you said "…"

I got lost after…

Trap check

I didn't hear any traps.

This part felt a little like a ramble because…

One signpost that would help is…

Build your talk: Throughline + Outline

30 minIndividual → PairsWorksheet F · Milestone

Goal: leave with a throughline of 15 words or fewer and a first outline. Due at the start of Week 4.

SectionThink about…
1 Throughline15 words or fewer. The word counter helps.
2 ChecklistTick the questions you can honestly say yes to.
3 My one listenerA real person: curious, smart, not an expert, someone you like.
4 OutlineOpening · Point 1 · Point 2 · (Point 3 for B2+) · Ending, each with a signpost.
5 Trap checkNo pitch, no ramble, no org bore, no performance.

A model

Throughline: Asking for help is a skill, not a weakness. (9 words)
Opening: "On my first day at college, I sat alone for two hours." · Point 1 (First,) why we don't ask · Point 2 (I also noticed…) what changed when I asked · Ending (So here's my question to you:) who will you ask this week?

Exit ticket

1. My throughline is…

2. The trap I need to watch out for is… because…

Before next class

  • Say your throughline out loud to one person. What was clear? What was missing?
  • Bring your outline to Week 4. You will add a story.
