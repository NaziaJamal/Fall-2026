Overview

The big idea for today

A great talk gives instead of takes, and every part of it follows one throughline.

Anderson's four traps show what goes wrong; his checklist tests the throughline. Matt Cutts shows a short talk built on one clear line with signposts at every turn. Students leave with their own throughline (15 words or fewer) and an outline.

By the end of class, students can…

  • Name and explain Anderson's four talk traps
  • Identify the throughline and outline of a short talk
  • Use signposts to introduce, order, add, move on and end
  • Test their own throughline with the checklist
  • Write a throughline of 15 words or fewer and a first outline

Session A · What goes wrong, and what goes right

1

Gift or Ask?

Engage6:00–6:15
GoalIntroduce Anderson's central test (gift or ask) through real-feeling openings
InteractionWhole class → pairs → whole class
MaterialsGift or Ask? warm-up file · worksheet A1
Teacher instructions
  1. 2 minOpen the warm-up file. Explain the vote: one hand = Gift, two hands = Ask.
    Last week we said a talk is a gift. Today: how do talks go wrong?
  2. 10 minFor each opening: read it aloud dramatically, students vote, pairs discuss "What's wrong? How would you fix it?" for 30 seconds, then press Reveal. Move fast; spend longer on 2–3 good discussions.
  3. 3 minDebrief: "What did the Ask openings have in common?" (about the speaker, selling, unprepared, fake emotion). Write students' words on the board; keep them for the reading.
Expected output: 8 votes with reasons; a board list that predicts the four traps.

B2 support

Students follow the openings on worksheet A1 while you read.

B2+ challenge

Rewrite one Ask opening as a Gift, out loud, in 20 seconds.

Four Traps Jigsaw + Bad Talk Charades

Study6:30–7:10
GoalUnderstand the four traps, then show them in action
InteractionPairs → expert groups → teaching groups → individual → group performance
MaterialsJigsaw cards · expert/notes sheet · charades cards · worksheet B1–B5
  1. 3 minPredict (B1). Show the chapter subtitle "Four Talk Styles to Avoid." Compare with the board list from the warm-up.
  2. 10 minExpert groups (B2). Groups 1–4 read one trap card. Trap 4 is for B2+. Check experts can explain without reading.
  3. 12 minTeaching groups (B3). One expert per trap; 2–3 minutes each; listeners complete the table.
  4. 5 minSpot the trap (B4). Individually, then check.
  5. 10 minBad Talk Charades (B5). Each teaching group draws a card (secret trap + topic) and plans for 3 minutes. Groups perform a 30-second terrible talk; the class guesses the trap; the group gives one fixed opening line. Keep it light and funny.
Key points
TrapWhat it isDo instead
1 Sales PitchUsing the talk to sell (the consultant story).Give, don't take (Bryan Stevenson).
2 RambleNo preparation, no direction.Prepare; respect the audience's time.
3 Org BoreHistory, structure, team and products of your organization.Talk about the work and the ideas.
4 Inspiration PerformancePerforming emotion and chasing applause, with little substance.Earn it: real work, shared with humility.
Expected output: completed notes table, B4 checked, 4–8 short charades with fixed openings.

B2 support

Cards 1–3; word help on each card; expert card with prompts.

B2+ challenge

Card 4. In charades, explain how your fixed line avoids the trap.
Homework option: the 2-page reading summary handout covers both chapters for students who missed class or want to review.

Watch & Analyze: Matt Cutts

Study7:10–7:40
GoalFind a short talk's throughline and map its outline
InteractionPairs → individual → groups → class
MaterialsCutts talk · worksheet C1–C6
  1. 2 minPredict (C1) from the title.
  2. 6 minViewing 1 (C2). Big picture: choose the throughline (c), then write it in ≤ 15 words. Pairs compare.
  3. 5 minViewing 2 (C3). Order the 7 parts. Show it's an outline: opening → idea → four lessons → question.
  4. 6 minViewing 3 (C4). Gap-fill, captions off. Then check.

5

Signposting

Study → Activate8:00–8:35
GoalUse signposts to help listeners follow a talk's structure
InteractionPairs → teacher-led → individual → pairs
MaterialsClass flow slides 10–11 · worksheet D1–D5 · study notes
  1. 6 minNotice (D1). Pairs match six Cutts signposts to their jobs.
    A reader can look back at the page. A listener can't. Signposts are the road signs of a talk.
  2. 7 minExplain (D2). Walk through the toolkit by job. Point out that spoken signposts are short and simple ("So, …", "Now, …"). Model the pause after a signpost.
  3. 6 minControlled (D3). Choose the best signpost in the cooking mini-talk; check.
  4. 8 minSemi-controlled (D4). Fix the ramble with at least four signposts; pairs compare, then show the model.
  5. 8 minFreer (D5). Students write four signposts for their own talk and say them aloud to a partner.
Expected output: D1 and D3 correct; a rewritten ramble; four personal signposts.

B2 support

Use the toolkit table; focus on first / also / finally / here's my question.

B2+ challenge

Add an emphasizing cleft ("What really matters is…") and a contrast ("But here's the surprising part…").
Common errors: "Firstly… Secondly…" (too formal for a talk) · previewing three points and giving two · "Finally" in the middle · "In conclusion I would like to conclude…"
6

Talk Map Trios

Activate8:35–9:10
GoalGive a structured 90-second talk that a listener can map
InteractionIndividual → trios (rotating roles) → new trios
MaterialsTimer · worksheet E1–E3 · slide 12
  1. 5 minPlan (E1). Draft throughline, 2–3 points and an ending, using their Week 2 idea.
  2. 15 minRound 1. Roles: Speaker (90 sec) · Mapper (writes throughline, points in order, signposts heard) · Listener (one question + trap check). Rotate so everyone speaks. About 5 minutes per speaker including feedback.
  3. 3 minCompare (E3). Speakers read the map of their talk. Did the map match the plan? Fix one thing.
  4. 12 minRound 2. New trios, 75 seconds, same roles. The aim: a map that matches the plan.
Expected output: each student speaks twice; two maps of their talk; one improvement.

B2 support

Speaker can hold a card with the throughline and signposts in round 1. Use feedback frames.

B2+ challenge

No notes in round 2. Listener asks a challenging question.

Throughline + Outline + Exit Ticket

Activate9:10–9:40
GoalComplete the Week 3 milestone
InteractionIndividual → pairs → individual
MaterialsWorksheet Part F · slides 13–14
By the end of today, you'll have one line that connects your whole talk, and a map to follow.
  1. 5 minThroughline (F1): 15 words or fewer; the online word counter helps. Then the checklist (F2).
  2. 3 minMy one listener (F3): Gilbert's advice to prepare for one real person.
  3. 12 minOutline (F4) with a signpost for each part, and a trap check (F5). Walk around: is every point linked to the throughline?
  4. 5 minPartner check: say the throughline aloud (Tenembaum's advice). Partner says what's clear and what's missing.
  5. 5 minExit ticket: "My throughline is…" / "The trap I need to watch out for is… because…"
Expected output: throughline + outline (Week 3 milestone) and an exit ticket from every student.

B2 support

Two points in the outline; model on the task card.

B2+ challenge

Three points, and the outline fits 6–7 minutes.
