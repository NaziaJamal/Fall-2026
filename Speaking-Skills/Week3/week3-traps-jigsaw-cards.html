Steps (40 min)

  1. Predict (3 min). "What makes a talk bad?" Pairs list ideas (worksheet B1).
  2. Expert groups (10 min). Groups 1–4 read their card and fill in the top half of page 4.
  3. Teaching groups (12 min). New groups of four (one from each trap). Each expert teaches for 2–3 minutes; others fill in the notes table.
  4. Spot the trap (5 min). Worksheet B4, then check.
  5. Bad Talk Charades (10 min). Each teaching group draws a card, plans a 30-second terrible talk (3 min), performs it, the class guesses the trap, and the group gives a fixed opening line.

1

The Sales Pitch

Trap card · Expert group 1

B2

The trap. Using a talk to take (sell yourself, your book, your business) instead of to give.

The story. A famous consultant promised TED a talk on creative thinking, then only listed companies that did well after hiring him. Anderson interrupted and asked him to share the actual method. The audience cheered, and the speaker finally did. The talk was never posted.

Why it fails. The audience feels used, and the speaker loses respect. Even small "ads" are risky.

Do instead. Be generous. Bryan Stevenson never asked for money in his TED talk, yet the audience gave his organization over $1.3 million.

Anderson's words"Give, don't take."
Word help
consultant: an expert companies pay for advice
restless: unable to sit still, impatient
generous: happy to give
injustice: unfair treatment
✂
2

The Ramble

Trap card · Expert group 2

B2

The trap. An unprepared talk that wanders with no clear direction.

The story. One early TED speaker admitted he'd planned his talk on the drive there, then shared loose thoughts about the future. People clapped politely, but nobody learned anything.

Why it fails. It tells the audience their time doesn't matter. Their time and attention are precious, and you can't give them back.

Do instead. Prepare. A great idea needs preparation time. After that talk, TED worked much harder with its speakers.

Anderson's words"Rambling is not an option."
Word help
wander: move without a clear direction
boast: talk proudly about yourself
improvise: do it without preparing
precious: very valuable
✂
3

The Org Bore

Trap card · Expert group 3

B2

The trap. A talk about your organization: its history, structure, team and products.

Why it fails. Your organization is fascinating to the people inside it, and boring to almost everyone else.

Compare. Boring: "In 2005 we opened a new department and put a vice president in charge of energy costs…" Interesting: "In 2005 we found a way for an ordinary office to cut energy costs by 60%. Here's how…"

Do instead. Talk about the work and the ideas behind it, not the organization.

Anderson's words"One mode is a gift. The other is lazily self-serving."
Word help
organization (org): a company or group
NGO: a nonprofit, non-government group
praise: say good things about
self-serving: helping only yourself
✂
4

The Inspiration Performance

Trap card · Expert group 4

B2+

The trap. Trying to look inspiring with big emotion and applause, but with little real content.

The story. A TED fan copied the "best bits" of famous talks. He paused for applause, used weak evidence and an edited photo, ran over time, and ended talking about dreams. Some people stood up, but later the audience said they felt manipulated.

Why it fails. Inspiration must be earned. Chasing it makes people pull back.

Do instead. Do real, meaningful work, then share what you learned with humility.

Anderson's words"Inspiration can't be performed."
Word help
prop: an object a speaker shows
manipulated: controlled unfairly
earned: deserved through effort
humility: not thinking you're better than others
