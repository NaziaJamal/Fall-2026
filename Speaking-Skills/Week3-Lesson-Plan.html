Teacher:Nazia Jamal Skill Focus/ Class:Speaking Skills – TED Talks: Ideas Worth Sharing (Proficient)
Week 3: Traps and Throughline · Signposting		 Level:B2 · B2+ Date:Week 3 · 6:00–9:40 pm
Lesson Objective: By the end of class, students will be able to (1) name and explain Anderson's four talk traps; (2) identify the throughline and outline of a short talk (Matt Cutts); (3) use signposts to introduce, order, add, move on, and end; (4) test their own throughline with Anderson's checklist; and (5) write a throughline of 15 words or fewer and a first outline of their own TED-style talk.
Syllabus links: SLO 1 (throughline and structure) · SLO 3 (one idea, throughline ≤ 15 words) · SLO 5 (signposting) · SLO 7 (peer feedback). Builds on the Week 2 Idea Blueprint; leads to the Week 4 story and the Week 6 midterm pitch.

1. Warm-upEngage

Gift or Ask?

Goal: Introduce Anderson's central test (does a talk give or take?) through realistic talk openings.

  1. 2 min Open the warm-up file. Explain the vote: one hand = Gift, two hands = Ask. Link to Week 2: “Last week we said a talk is a gift. Today: how do talks go wrong?”
  2. 10 min For each of the 8 openings: T reads it aloud dramatically → Ss vote → pairs discuss “What's wrong? How would you fix it?” (30 sec) → T presses Reveal. Keep the pace fast; spend longer on 2–3 good discussions.
  3. 3 min Debrief: “What did the Ask openings have in common?” (about the speaker, selling, unprepared, fake emotion). Write Ss' words on the board and keep them for the reading.

Expected output: 8 votes with reasons; a board list that predicts the four traps.

B2 support: Ss follow the openings on worksheet A1 while T reads.

B2+ challenge: Rewrite one Ask opening as a Gift, out loud, in 20 seconds.

2. VocabularyEngage

Words from the Book and the Talk

Goal: Pre-teach 14 words and phrases needed for the reading and the video.

  1. 7 min Vocabulary cards: play audio, drill stress, ask concept-check questions (e.g., “If I steer clear of sugar, do I eat it?” “Did I prepare if I winged it?”). Use “Hide meaning” for 2–3 words.
  2. 5 min Pairs match words and meanings (A2), then click Check answers.
  3. 3 min Ss complete two A3 sentences about themselves and share with a partner.

Expected output: A2 matching correct; two personal sentences per student.

B2 support: Focus on the high-use items (takeaway, ramble, wing it, give it a shot); meanings visible on cards.

B2+ challenge: Use three of the words in a 20-second comment about a talk they have seen.

3. Guided readingStudy

Four Traps Jigsaw + Bad Talk Charades

Goal: Understand Anderson's four talk traps and the throughline checklist, then show the traps in action.

  1. 3 min Predict (B1): show the chapter subtitle “Four Talk Styles to Avoid.” Ss compare it with the board list from the warm-up.
  2. 10 min Expert groups (B2): groups 1–4 each read one trap card (1 Sales Pitch · 2 Ramble · 3 Org Bore · 4 Inspiration Performance). T checks that experts can explain without reading.
  3. 12 min Teaching groups (B3): one expert per trap, 2–3 min each; listeners complete the notes table (what it is / do instead).
  4. 5 min Spot the trap (B4): individually, then check as a class.
  5. 10 min Bad Talk Charades (B5): each teaching group draws a card (secret trap + topic), plans for 3 min, then performs a 30-second terrible talk. The class guesses the trap; the group gives one fixed opening line. Keep it light and funny.

Expected output: Completed notes table; B4 checked; 4–8 short charades with fixed openings.

B2 support: Cards 1–3 (easier traps); word help on each card; expert card with prompts.

B2+ challenge: Card 4 (Inspiration Performance). In charades, explain how the fixed line avoids the trap.

 Pairs → expert groups → teaching groups → individual → group performance

4. Watch & analyzeStudy

Matt Cutts: Watch It Three Ways

Goal: Identify a short talk's throughline and map its outline and signposts.

  1. 2 min Predict (C1) the talk's content from the title.
  2. 6 min Viewing 1 – big picture (C2): choose the throughline, then write it in 15 words or fewer. Pairs compare.
  3. 5 min Viewing 2 – structure (C3): put the 7 parts in order. Show that it is an outline: opening → idea → four lessons → question to the audience.
  4. 6 min Viewing 3 – language (C4): gap-fill with captions off, then check.
  5. 8 min Groups (C5): answer Anderson's checklist questions about the talk, with evidence.
  6. 3 min Class (C6): quick round – “What would you try for 30 days?”

Expected output: Throughline in 15 words or fewer; outline in order; checklist answered with evidence.

B2 support: Captions on for Viewing 1; frame: “His talk is about… and his main message is…”

B2+ challenge: Evaluate: is the novel example a gift or a little self-promotion? Defend your answer.

 Pairs → individual → groups → whole class

5. Language focusStudy → Activate

Signposting: Road Signs for Your Audience

Goal: Use signposts to introduce, order, add, move on, and end so listeners can follow a talk's structure.

  1. 6 min Notice (D1): pairs match six Cutts signposts to their jobs. Point out: “A reader can look back at the page. A listener can't. Signposts are the road signs of a talk.”
  2. 7 min Explain (D2): walk through the toolkit by job. Spoken signposts are short and simple (“So, …” “Now, …”). Model the pause after a signpost.
  3. 6 min Controlled (D3): choose the best signpost in the cooking mini-talk; check.
  4. 8 min Semi-controlled (D4): fix the rambling talk with at least four signposts; pairs compare, then show the model.
  5. 8 min Freer (D5): Ss write four signposts for their own talk and say them aloud to a partner.

Expected output: D1 and D3 correct; a rewritten ramble; four personal signposts. Watch for: “Firstly… Secondly…” (too formal), previewing three points and giving two, “Finally” in the middle, “In conclusion I would like to conclude…”

B2 support: Use the toolkit table; focus on first / also / finally / here's my question.

B2+ challenge: Add an emphasizing cleft (“What really matters is…”) and a contrast (“But here's the surprising part…”).

6. Speaking practiceActivate

Talk Map Trios

Goal: Give a structured 90-second talk that a listener can map.

  1. 5 min Plan (E1): Ss draft a throughline, 2–3 points, and an ending based on their Week 2 idea.
  2. 15 min Round 1: roles – Speaker (90 sec) · Mapper (writes throughline, points in order, signposts heard) · Listener (one question + trap check). Rotate so everyone speaks (~5 min per speaker including feedback).
  3. 3 min Compare (E3): speakers read the map of their talk. Did it match the plan? Fix one thing.
  4. 12 min Round 2: new trios, 75 seconds, same roles. Aim: a map that matches the plan.

Expected output: Each student speaks twice; two maps of their talk; one improvement made between rounds.

B2 support: Speaker may hold a card with the throughline and signposts in Round 1; listeners use feedback frames.

B2+ challenge: No notes in Round 2; listener asks a challenging question.

 Individual → trios (rotating roles) → new trios

7. Build your talk + exit ticketActivate

Throughline + Outline

Goal: Complete the Week 3 milestone: “By the end of today, you'll have one line that connects your whole talk, and a map to follow.”

  1. 5 min Throughline (F1): 15 words or fewer (online word counter helps), then test it with Anderson's checklist (F2).
  2. 3 min My one listener (F3): prepare for one real person (Gilbert's advice).
  3. 12 min Outline (F4) with a signpost for each part, plus a trap check (F5). T circulates: is every point linked to the throughline?
  4. 5 min Partner check: say the throughline aloud (Tenembaum's advice); partner says what is clear and what is missing.
  5. 5 min Exit ticket: “My throughline is…” / “The trap I need to watch out for is… because…”

Expected output: Throughline + outline (Week 3 milestone) and an exit ticket from every student.

B2 support: Two points in the outline; model on the task card.

B2+ challenge: Three points, and the outline fits a 6–7 minute talk.
