1What is the midterm pitch?

In Week 6, you stand up and pitch the idea for your final talk: what your idea is, why it matters, and how your talk will build it. Think of it as a short trailer for your final TED-style talk.

2How to build your pitch

Use what you made in Weeks 1–5: your idea (Week 2), your throughline and outline (Week 3), your story (Week 4) and your explanation and persuasion (Week 5).

B2 · about 2 minutes

Hook15 sec
Throughline + why it matters25 sec
2 main points + a short story or example60 sec
Ending15 sec
Buffer5 sec

B2+ · about 3 minutes

Hook20 sec
Throughline + why it matters30 sec
2–3 main points, with a story, explanation or evidence100 sec
Ending20 sec
Buffer10 sec

What each part does

PartGoalUseful language
HookMake the audience curious in the first seconds. A question, a surprising fact, or the first line of a story.Have you ever…? · Three years ago, I… · Here's something surprising: …
ThroughlineSay your idea in one clear sentence (15 words or fewer). Then say why it matters to this audience.What I want you to understand is… · The reason this matters is that…
Main pointsPreview your points, then give each one. Support at least one with a short story, example or piece of evidence.I'll share two things… · First, … · For example, … · That brings me to…
EndingBring it back to your throughline and leave the audience with something: a question, a challenge, an image.So, to sum up, … · So here's my question to you: …

A model pitch (B2, about 2 minutes)

Hook: "On my first day at college, I sat alone in the library for two hours, too shy to ask where my class was."
Throughline: "What I've learned is that asking for help is a skill, not a weakness. This matters because almost everyone in this room is new to something."
Points: "I'll share two things. First, why we don't ask: we think it makes us look weak. For example, … Second, what changed when I started asking: …"
Ending: "So here's my question to you: who will you ask for help this week?"

Do

  • One idea, one throughline
  • Signposts at every turn
  • A real story or example
  • Look at the audience, not your notes
  • Practice with a timer

Avoid the four traps

  • Sales pitch: selling yourself or a product
  • Ramble: no plan, no clear direction
  • Org bore: all about your company or school
  • Inspiration performance: big emotion, little substance

Practice plan

  1. Write key words, not a script. One card per part: hook, throughline, points, ending.
  2. Say your throughline out loud to someone outside class. Can they repeat it?
  3. Time yourself three times. Cut anything that doesn't support your throughline.
  4. Record yourself on your phone once. Watch it: Where do you look at your notes? Where do you rush?
  5. Practice for one person you like: curious, smart, not an expert.

6On the day

Before you start

  • Arrive on time; pitches start at the beginning of class.
  • Have your note cards ready (B2) or put them away (B2+).
  • Breathe slowly. Stand with both feet on the floor.
  • Find one friendly face in the room to start with.

While you speak

  • Pause after your hook. Let it land.
  • Look at different people, one sentence each.
  • If you forget a word, pause, look at your card, continue.
  • Slow down for your throughline.

As a listener

You will give classmates feedback. Be specific and kind:

Your throughline was clear when you said…

Your story about ___ made me care because…

One thing that would make it stronger is…

7Questions

What if I can't come in Week 6?

You may present at the next session only if you tell your teacher before Week 6 and bring documentation. Talk to your teacher as early as possible.

Can I read my pitch?

No. B2 students may use note cards with key words; B2+ students use minimal notes. Reading a full script lowers your delivery score.

What if I go over or under time?

A few seconds is fine. A pitch that is much shorter or longer than your level's target loses points in delivery. Practice with a timer.

Can I change my idea after the midterm?

Yes, you can improve it. The feedback you get in Week 6 is there to help you make your final talk stronger. If you want to change it completely, talk to your teacher first.

Will my pitch be recorded?

If it is, the recording is for feedback only and is never shared outside the class without your permission.
