Narrative Tenses + Time Phrases · Study Notes

Week 4 · Connection and Story · How to tell a story your audience can follow

1Why tenses matter in a story

A listener can't go back and reread. Tenses and time phrases tell them when each thing happened, so they can follow your story in their head. Good storytellers don't always tell events in time order. They often start at an exciting moment, then go back in time to explain.

2The story toolkit

Past simpleverb + -ed / irregularthe main events, the "spine" of the story
Adichie
Then one Saturday, we went to his village to visit, and his mother showed us a beautifully patterned basket.

Keep the main events in the past simple and in order. This is the line your audience follows.

Past continuouswas / were + verb-ingthe scene; an action in progress when something happened
I was waiting outside the office when a man sat down next to me.
The rain was coming down, and everyone was running for cover.

Use it to "paint the background," then interrupt it with a past simple event: was …ing + when + past simple.

Past perfecthad + past participlea flashback; something earlier than the main story; a first time
Adichie
I had never been outside Nigeria. · It had not occurred to me that anybody in his family could actually make something. · She had felt sorry for me even before she saw me.
Anderson's boat story
It turned out that Dad knew the boat was going to sink, and he had called them with our exact location.

Perfect for surprises and realizations: the audience learns about an earlier action at the moment you want. Don't use it for every verb, only when you go back in time.

would / used towould + verb · used to + verbrepeated habits in the past
Adichie
When I didn't finish my dinner, my mother would say, "Finish your food!"
Every summer, we used to visit my grandmother by the sea.

used to also works for past states (I used to live in Cairo). would does not (✗ I would live in Cairo).

was going to / was about towas going to + verb · was about to + verba plan or a prediction, seen from the past
Anderson's boat story
Dad knew the boat was going to sink.
I was about to give up when my phone rang.

Great for tension: something almost happened.

4Say it like a storyteller

  1. Pause after a time phrase. "Then one Saturday | we went to his village." The pause tells the audience: something new is coming.
  2. Use contractions. In speech, say I'd never seen…, she'd already left, we were about to…. It sounds natural, not written.
  3. Slow down for the key line. Say it clearly, then pause. "Do you trust me, son?" |
  4. Show, don't list. One vivid detail ("my shoes were full of water") is better than five facts (the date, the bus number, the price).
  5. Look up at the moment that matters. Make eye contact before your first word, and again at the key line.
B2+ · The historic present. Some storytellers switch to the present tense at the most exciting moment to make it feel live: "So I'm standing at the bus stop. The bus is coming. I raise my hand… and it drives right past me." Use it for one scene only, then return to the past.

5Common errors

✗ Don't say✓ SayWhy
I have seen him yesterday.I saw him yesterday.Finished time (yesterday, in 2019) needs the past simple.
When I arrived, the party finished. (= it ended before)When I arrived, the party had finished.The past perfect shows it happened earlier.
I had woken up, I had showered, and I had taken the bus…I woke up, showered, and took the bus…Events in order = past simple. Save the past perfect for flashbacks.
I was knowing the answer.I knew the answer.know, want, believe, need are not usually continuous.
When I was child, I would live in a small town.When I was a child, I used to live in a small town.would is for repeated actions, not states.
