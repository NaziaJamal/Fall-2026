Overview

The big idea for today

Connect before your first word. Then take the audience on a journey: one true story, with a point.

Chapter 5 shows how speakers win trust in the first seconds (eye contact and a real smile). Chapter 6 explains why stories work and what makes them great. Adichie models three short personal stories that all serve one throughline. Students leave with a 2-minute story for their own talk, rehearsed with a proper "walk-in."

By the end of class, students can…

  • Open with eye contact, a pause, and a natural smile before the first word
  • Explain why stories hold attention and name Anderson's four key things
  • Analyze how Adichie builds her stories (character, tension, detail, resolution, key line)
  • Use the past simple, past continuous, past perfect, would / used to, and time phrases to tell a story
  • Plan, cut, and rehearse a true 2-minute story that links to their throughline

1

The First Five Seconds

Engage6:00–6:15
GoalExperience how much eye contact and a real smile change trust, before reading Ch. 5
InteractionWhole class mingle → pairs → whole class
MaterialsFirst Five Seconds file (built-in timers) · worksheet A1 · task card 1
Teacher instructions
  1. 2 minEveryone stands. Show the first screen.
    Before a speaker says a word, you have already decided: friend or foe? Confident or tentative? Let's test it.
  2. 6 minThree mingle rounds (45 sec each + rating). Each round, students greet two people and say "Hi! I have a story for you." Round 1: eyes on their phone. Round 2: eyes over the other person's head. Round 3: stop, two seconds of eye contact, nod, a real smile, then speak. After each round, students show their trust rating with 1–5 fingers and note it in A1.
  3. 3 min"Try not to smile." Pairs: A keeps a straight face; B describes the best meal of their life with a big, real smile (30 sec). Swap. It's very hard not to smile back; this is Anderson's "our minds sync."
  4. 4 minDebrief with the four questions. Click Reveal Anderson's advice at the end: walk on and pause → pick out a couple of people → eye contact, nod, smile → then begin.
Expected output: three trust ratings (usually rising sharply in Round 3), a list of first-seconds clues, and a prediction for the reading.

B2 support

Model each round yourself first with a volunteer. Write the line on the board.

B2+ challenge

In the debrief, explain why Round 2 can feel worse than Round 1 (the speaker seems to be looking "through" you).

Story Stations + Story Rescue

Study6:30–7:10
GoalUnderstand Ch. 5 (eye contact) and Ch. 6 (narration), then apply the four key things to a bad story
InteractionPairs → groups rotating around the room → groups perform
MaterialsStation posters A–E · worksheet B1–B3 · Story Rescue cards · slide timer
  1. 3 minPredict (B1). Show the chapter title "Narration: The Irresistible Allure of Stories." Pairs: why do humans love stories?
  2. 25 minStation rotation (B2). Groups of 3–4 start at different letters; 5 minutes per station (slide 6, "Start 5:00"). Roles rotate: Reader, Writer, Timekeeper, Reporter. On the bell, move to the next letter (E → A). Circulate: check groups are discussing, not just copying.
  3. 5 minCheck. Reporters share one answer per station; show slide 7 (walk-in steps + four key things). Groups press Check stations.
  4. 7 minStory Rescue (B3). Each group gets a badly told story card. Plan 3 min (slide 8 timer), then each group tells its rescued story in 45 seconds. The class guesses one thing they cut. Keep it fast and fun.
Expected output: completed station passport (B2), answers checked, 4 rescued stories performed with a key line and a new ending.

B2 support

Readers share the paragraphs; use the word-help box first. Give B2-heavy groups Rescue cards 1 or 3 (shorter).

B2+ challenge

Station E question 3 (parable risks). In Story Rescue, use one past perfect flashback and end without saying the moral directly.

Homework option: the reading summary covers Ch. 5–6 for students who missed class or want to review.

Study7:10–7:40
GoalAnalyze how a speaker connects and builds short stories around one throughline
InteractionPairs → individual → groups → class
MaterialsAdichie talk (0:00–6:26) · worksheet C1–C6 · slides 9–10
  1. 2 minPredict (C1) from the title: what is a "single story"?
  2. 8 minViewing 1 (C2). First 0:00–0:20 with the sound off: what do her eyes and face do? (Link to Station A.) Then play 0:00–6:26 with sound and captions on. Students complete the three-story table and choose the throughline. Pairs compare; check with slide 10.
  3. 5 minViewing 2 (C3). Replay the Fide story, 2:50–4:04. Order the seven parts, then find the four key things and the key line.

  5. 6 minGroups (C5). Evaluate with Anderson's advice: strongest story, what the stories give us, humor, structure.
  6. 3 minClass (C6). Quick round: "Have you ever had a single story about a place, a job, or a group of people?"

Narrative Tenses + Human Timeline

Study → Activate8:00–8:35
GoalUse narrative tenses and time phrases to control the order of a story
InteractionPairs → teacher-led → individual → groups (movement) → pairs
MaterialsSlides 12–13 · worksheet D1–D5 · study notes · Human Timeline cards
  1. 6 minNotice (D1). Pairs match six sentences from Adichie and Anderson to their jobs.
    A listener can't reread. Tenses and time phrases tell them when each thing happened.
  2. 7 minExplain (D2). Use slide 12 and the study notes timeline: past perfect (back story) → past continuous (scene) → past simple (events). Highlight would for habits, was about to for tension. Model the pause after a time phrase and contractions (I'd never…).
  3. 6 minControlled (D3). "Every Conversation Is an Interview": choose the correct forms; check.
  4. 8 minHuman Timeline (D4, movement). Hand out one card per student (groups of 6–7 per story). Round 1: line up in time order and tell it in the past simple. Round 2: the ★ person steps forward; re-line in story order (★ first, then back in time, then the ending) and say each sentence again with the right tense and a time phrase. Groups then write the story in D4 (finish for homework if needed).
  5. 8 minFreer (D5). Students write the first four sentences of their own true story and say them to a partner, pausing after each time phrase.
Expected output: D1 and D3 correct; two Human Timeline retellings; four personal sentences with a time phrase, past continuous, past perfect, and a key event.

B2 support

In Round 2, write had + past participle and was + -ing on the board. Allow students to read from the card.

B2+ challenge

Try the historic present for the ★ moment ("So I'm standing at the desk…") and return to the past for the ending.

Common errors: present perfect with finished time ("I have seen him yesterday") · past perfect for every verb · would for states ("I would live in…") · stative verbs in the continuous ("I was knowing").

Cut the Fat + Campfire Circles

Activate8:35–9:10
GoalTell a short story three times, cutting it to the essentials, then perform the best version
InteractionIndividual → two lines (rotating) → circles of four → class
MaterialsPrompt cards · Story Scorecard · worksheet E1–E3 · slides 14–15 (timer buttons) · LED candle or phone flashlight
  1. 5 minPlan (E1). Students take a prompt card or use a story for their own talk. Notes only: WHO · TROUBLE · THE MOMENT · THE END · SO WHAT?
  2. 13 minCut the Fat (E2, movement). Two lines facing each other. Before speaking, each teller looks, pauses, and smiles. Round 1: 90 sec each + one comment. One line moves left. Round 2: 75 sec. Move. Round 3: 60 sec. Same story every time; it must get shorter and stronger. Use the slide 14 timer buttons.
  3. 12 minCampfire Circles (E3). Dim the lights. Groups of four sit around a "campfire." Each person tells their best 60–75 sec version; listeners score with 1–3 fingers on the Story Scorecard and give one comment. The circle chooses a Campfire Champion.
  4. 5 minChampions. Two or three champions tell their story to the whole class. The class says what the point was.
Expected output: each student tells the same story four times; it gets shorter and clearer each time; three pieces of feedback noted in E2.

B2 support

Allow the E1 note card in Rounds 1–2. Use the B2 feedback frames on the scorecard.

B2+ challenge

No notes from Round 2. Give feedback without frames: specific and diplomatic ("I wonder if…", "You might consider…").

Your 2-minute story + Walk-in Rehearsal + Exit Ticket

Activate9:10–9:40
GoalComplete the Week 4 milestone: a true 2-minute story that links to the throughline
InteractionIndividual → pairs → individual
MaterialsWorksheet Part F · model story map "The Umbrella" (task card 7) · slides 16–17
By the end of today, you'll have a true story that your audience will remember, and it will carry your throughline.
  1. 5 minStory finder (F1). Students copy their Week 3 throughline and list three true moments. Four tests: true? links to the throughline? fits in 2 minutes? gives the audience something?
  2. 10 minStory map (F2). Show the model ("The Umbrella"). Students complete their map: where it goes in the outline, character + goal, tension, two vivid details, key line, resolution, point, cut list. Circulate: is the point linked to the throughline?
  3. 10 minWalk-in rehearsal (F4). Pairs. The speaker walks to the partner, stops, makes eye contact, nods, smiles, then tells the story (2 min max; slide 17 timer). The partner times it and gives one thing that worked + one thing to cut. Swap.
  4. 5 minExit ticket. Key line · what the story gives · one detail to cut · readiness 1–5.
Expected output: a story map, a timed first telling with a proper walk-in, and an exit ticket from every student. Scripts (F3) are finished for homework.

B2 support

Bullet notes, not a script. Use the model's structure directly. 1½–2 minutes is fine.

B2+ challenge

Full script (about 250–300 words) with a past perfect flashback; end with a line that "lets it mean something" without force-feeding the moral.

Weekly milestone · Your 2-minute story

What students submit

  • Worksheet Part F (story finder, story map, story), due at the start of Week 5
  • Online: Download answers or Save as PDF · Paper: printed Part F
  • B2: story map + bullet notes, 1½–2 min · B2+: full script, about 250–300 words, 2 min
  • Counts toward weekly milestones (25%)

Before next class

  • Tell the story out loud three times with a timer, cutting until it fits
  • Tell it once to a real person, looking at them before the first word
  • Why: in Week 5, students build a persuasive outline around their story; in Week 6, it can open the midterm pitch
Assessment link: the story is the student's first talk tool (SLO 4: B2 needs at least two in the final talk, B2+ at least three). Narrative tenses support SLO 5. The walk-in (eye contact before the first word) is part of delivery (SLO 6) and is assessed at the Week 6 midterm pitch and the Week 11 final talk.

D4 · Human Timeline: model answer (Story A, story order)

I finally reached the check-in desk, put my hand in my jacket pocket, and… nothing. My passport wasn't there. Months earlier, I had booked a cheap flight to visit my grandmother. The night before, I had put my passport in my jacket pocket. That morning, I had taken a taxi to the airport at 5 a.m., and I had been waiting in a long line for almost an hour. Just then, a woman behind me tapped me on the shoulder and held up a passport. Mine. It had fallen out while we were waiting in line. Since then, I've sent her a postcard every year.
