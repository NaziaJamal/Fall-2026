Too Many Choices

Adapted for Current Issues · Basic Level · Story A

Go to a supermarket. There are many kinds of cereal. There are many kinds of bread and jam. Is this good? Some scientists say no.

Barry Schwartz is a scientist. He writes about people and their choices. He says too many choices can make people stressed. People are afraid to make a bad choice. After they choose, they think, “Maybe the other one is better.”

There is a famous study about jam. In a store, there are two tables. One table has 24 kinds of jam. The other table has 6 kinds of jam. More people buy jam from the small table! Many choices are hard. A few choices are easy.

What can you do? Make a short list of what you need. Choose a good option. It does not need to be perfect. You can also ask a friend for help.

More Choices, More Stress?

Adapted for Current Issues · Basic Level · Story B

Walk into any supermarket and there are dozens of kinds of cereal, bread, and jam. Many people think more choices give us more freedom. But some behavioral scientists say that too many options can cause anxiety.

Barry Schwartz is a psychology professor and the writer of a book called The Paradox of Choice. He says that when there are many options, people worry about making the wrong decision. After they choose, they often feel regret and ask, “Is another option better?” This is especially hard for “maximizers” — people who always want the best possible choice.

Studies show the same thing. In one famous study, a store has two tables. One table has 24 kinds of jam, and the other has only 6. More shoppers buy jam from the smaller table. In another study, companies offer savings plans to their workers. When there are more investment options, fewer workers join the plan.

Daniel Willingham, a psychology professor at the University of Virginia, explains that our brain likes routines. Making a decision takes a lot of mental energy, so we often choose the same things again and again.

So what can we do? Experts give three tips: decide your criteria before you shop, choose an option that is “good enough” and not perfect, and ask someone you trust for advice.

Help words: behavioral scientist = a person who studies how people act · anxiety = a strong worried feeling · regret = feeling bad about a choice · maximizer = a person who always wants the best · savings plan / investment = a way to save money for the future · routine = things you do every day the same way · criteria = the things that are important to you
